# Plain-language change workflow learning review

> Fail small enough to remain safe, fast enough to remain useful and openly enough that others can learn.

## Context

- **Change, test or event:** Development of Foundations v0.2 and v0.3 through an AI-led repository workflow.
- **Intended outcome or learning:** Determine whether Jamie could lead through problems and decisions while AI handled technical execution.
- **People and services affected:** Jamie and the Operations Automated repository only.
- **Owner:** Jamie Peppard.

## Boundaries and recovery

- **Permitted scope and consequence:** Non-confidential methodology content and reversible GitHub changes.
- **Failure signals and observation method:** Jamie cannot explain the current position, next step or decision required; repeated questions are needed to recover programme context.
- **Stop conditions:** Unclear authority, confidential information, external publication, a requested connection, material spending or a destructive unrecoverable action.
- **Recovery or rollback method:** Draft pull requests, version history, explicit approval gates and reversible branch operations.
- **Legal, safety, ethical and human obligations:** Confidentiality boundary, human approval of authoritative meaning and no inferred authority for external or consequential action.

## What happened

The repository changes were technically controlled and traceable. Nevertheless, Jamie did not have a usable view of the project plan and had to ask what was happening and what he needed to do. The process optimised the mechanics of change before consistently presenting the programme in the form the user needed.

The failure remained bounded: no confidential information was introduced, no external publication occurred, no connection or spending was authorised, and all repository changes remained reviewable and recoverable.

## Review without default blame

- The workflow assumed that a completed pull request and a concise summary would create sufficient understanding.
- The assistant retained more programme context than it exposed to Jamie.
- The repository did not initially contain a roadmap, so the plan depended too heavily on conversation history.
- “What happens next?” was treated as a local workflow question before it was treated as a product and programme question.
- Jamie's repeated questions were useful failure signals revealing a system design problem, not a lack of technical knowledge to correct.

## Retained learning

| Type | Lesson, decision or improvement | Owner | Due date or status | Where retained |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lesson | Technical completion does not create user clarity unless the outcome, phase and control point are made visible | AI execution workflow | Retained | This review and the pilot record |
| Decision | Validate the method through two pilots before defining product technology | Jamie Peppard | Approved in the v0.3 roadmap | `ROADMAP.md` |
| Improvement | Use a five-part milestone handoff for every material work cycle | AI execution workflow | Begin immediately | `operate-record.md` |
| Improvement | Propose a concise current-status record and reusable handoff template | AI execution workflow | Foundation v0.4 candidate | `operate-record.md` |
| Improvement | Ask Jamie for only one non-confidential pilot problem after presenting Pilot 1 findings | AI execution workflow | Next human control point | `ROADMAP.md` and this review |

## Next OPERATE step

- **Return to Redesign:** If Pilot 2 reveals that the templates or governance create disproportionate burden.
