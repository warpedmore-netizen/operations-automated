# Plain-language main answers and informed feedback actions

## Jamie's exact requirement

> Main answers must be written for the user in plain language. Internal source paths, statuses and control mechanics should stay behind optional details. Feedback actions and the guide must explain what will happen before asking the user to act.

Jamie authorised preparation and implementation on a proposal branch. He did not authorise merge, release, publication, spending, a new connection, risk acceptance or wider delegated authority.

## Recorded evidence

- A Workbench answer repeated a long user statement, named internal source files and statuses in the answer, described its internal synthesis method and presented change-control actions before returning a simple response to Jamie's reasoning.
- The retained Workbench proposal records the exact wording above, the originating conversation, a preparation decision and the approved output and evolution sources used at the time.
- The current Workbench already retained source and authority metadata, but its answer-feedback choices and guide did not consistently explain their result before the click.

## Jamie's judgement

The answer and action language should be written for the person using the Workbench. Technical trace and governance controls remain necessary, but they should not become the main reading experience.

## AI interpretation and counter-test

The strongest reasonable interpretation is progressive disclosure, not concealment: lead with the decision-useful answer and keep trace, status and enforcement mechanics inspectable. A consequential human decision remains visible in plain language even when the implementation mechanics sit behind optional detail.

The strongest alternative was to correct only the originating answer. That would preserve the same failure in future model responses, local responses, answer feedback and the guide. The issue therefore transfers across the Workbench delivery journey and justifies a reusable product correction.

## Methodology comparison and disposition

The approved output contract already requires plain English, prominent current outputs, inspectable derivation and an explanation of what follows an approval or transition. The approved Human-AI Collaboration Method already prefers comprehension and progressive depth over ceremonial completeness. The methodology meaning is sufficient; product delivery had not consistently enacted it.

Disposition: **no approved methodology wording change; correct the proposed Workbench product now and retain this as evidence for later methodology review if repeated use exposes a genuine gap.**

## Product response prepared

- lead the visible answer with plain-language meaning and the next action;
- remove or translate raw internal paths, status lines and delivery labels when rendering older or provider-generated answers;
- put sources, statuses, processing route and authority controls in one optional detail panel;
- retain exact source snapshots and control metadata without a SQLite schema change;
- explain the saved result beside every answer-feedback choice before selection;
- explain the selected treatment before saving feedback and keep change-review creation separate; and
- update the guide so sending, saving, creating a review, preparing and releasing each state their effect before action.

## Boundaries and review trigger

This proposal does not change approved methodology meaning or remove provenance, uncertainty, human authority or feedback retention. Review again after Jamie uses the corrected answer and feedback journeys on real work, or earlier if progressive disclosure hides a material decision, risk or limitation.
