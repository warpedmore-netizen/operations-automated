# Move from the primary journey into the operational value system

## Source and boundary

- **Source:** Jamie Peppard's clarification of the proposed outside-in operating rule.
- **Permission to use:** Jamie asked for this thinking to be built into the evolving methodology.
- **Confidentiality boundary:** General methodology-design feedback only. No employer, client or third-party information is included.

## Signal

Starting with the primary customer, service user or stakeholder journey is necessary but incomplete. It establishes why improvement matters and helps locate a bottleneck, failed outcome or unmet need. Once that is understood, the method should move one level inward and start at the beginning of the operational work that delivers the product, service or process.

The operation should make explicit:

- what enters the work;
- what the operation needs to do from beginning to end;
- which outcomes must happen;
- which work, decisions, information, controls, technology and people make them happen;
- what varies, fails or needs recovery;
- how consistent performance is observed; and
- where improvement feeds back into the service or product.

Operations is the point at which a promise becomes a repeatable outcome. Strong operational performance sustains value and creates capacity for new products, features and improvement. Falling performance can propagate into customer harm, regulatory exposure, rework, cost, workforce pressure and blocked change.

## Method-selection clarification

Operations Automated should treat established methodologies as a selectable toolkit rather than competing systems that require exclusive adoption.

The method may, for example:

- use a customer or service-user journey to establish purpose and experience;
- use Lean techniques to understand flow, delay, waste or failure demand;
- use MoSCoW to make the current scope and outcome priorities explicit;
- use ITIL, IT4IT, quality, risk, project or sector practices for deeper structure where suitable; and
- combine or adapt these methods when the resulting approach is understandable, proportionate and useful.

The selection must be based on the situation, service, product, process, consequence and capability of the people using it. Combining more tools is not automatically more rigorous.

## Scope and interface clarification

Operations Automated is concerned with an operational function and with the operational side of work wherever it occurs. It does not attempt to govern every part of Finance, People, Risk, Legal, Technology or another specialist profession.

Those capabilities should remain autonomous within their authority while supporting the end-to-end operational outcome through explicit, proportionate interfaces. They can also apply Operations Automated to improve their own operational work.

Risk and assurance should help a team understand material exposure early and improve before large remediation is required. When an unexplained or disproportionate control blocks delivery, the delay, pressure and secondary risk should become visible and be resolved through the appropriate authority. This does not permit delivery pressure to override an applicable obligation or allow a team to accept risk outside its authority.

## Challenge and boundary

“Start with Operations” should not become an inward-looking process exercise. The primary journey remains the reason and test for the operational design.

“Get Operations right and everything else follows” is directionally useful but not absolute. Good operations creates a stable platform for value and change, while product quality, strategy, finance, people, legal duties and other capabilities can still independently constrain or damage outcomes. The methodology should therefore centre the operational value system without claiming that Operations controls the whole organisation.

MoSCoW is useful where four priority categories create clarity. It is not a universal scoring method, and an applicable legal, safety, ethical or minimum-outcome obligation cannot be made optional by calling it a “Could” or “Won't”.

## Proposed disposition

Add a journey-to-operation sequence to the v0.7 operational coverage model and reader guide. Update the cross-functional interface model, practice-guide standard and draft coverage tool so that:

1. the primary journey establishes purpose and the point of investigation;
2. the method then maps the end-to-end operational value system from its first event;
3. required outcomes, priorities, minimum outcomes and exclusions are explicit;
4. methods are selected and combined deliberately for the context;
5. interfaces are proportionate and do not imply governance over whole specialist functions; and
6. risk and assurance enable early, proportionate control while retaining obligations and authority.
