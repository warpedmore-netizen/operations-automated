# Consistent method with tailored depth

## Source and boundary

- **Source:** Jamie Peppard's answer to the irreducible-core challenge.
- **Question tested:** Whether every user should complete the same Operations Automated workflow or whether one reasoning core should be applied proportionately.
- **Permission to use:** Jamie continued the founder methodology challenge and asked for the system to develop from the answers.
- **Confidentiality boundary:** General project and methodology direction only.

## Strongest contextual interpretation

Operations Automated is one methodology. Its principles and reasoning do not change merely because a user wants a shorter answer, but the depth, interaction and returned output should match the person's need, context and chosen level of engagement.

The delivery system should support at least two entry modes:

1. **Ask:** the user asks an operational question and receives an immediate, proportionate methodology-based answer.
2. **Assess or project:** the user begins a structured, consultancy-style analysis that collects and tests enough evidence to produce fuller operational artefacts, decisions and an implementation route.

The user should not need to understand or manually complete every methodology component in either mode. AI applies the relevant reasoning and explains what it used.

## Limitation and caveat

A quick answer must say what level of analysis it represents. It should identify:

- The information and assumptions on which it is based
- Which material areas have not been assessed
- The confidence and important uncertainty
- Whether the answer is operational guidance rather than a full assessment
- When specialist, regulated or authorised judgement is required
- What deeper analysis would enable

The caveat should be specific to the question. A generic disclaimer must not excuse a misleading answer or replace useful reasoning.

## Consistent minimum response

Even a short answer should return proportionately:

- Current understanding of the question and desired outcome
- Relevant evidence, user judgement, AI inference and assumptions
- The most important value, people, dependency, risk or authority considerations
- A useful answer or recommendation
- Limitations and the conditions under which the answer may change
- The next action and whether a deeper assessment is advisable

This is the same reasoning core as a full project, compressed to the decision the user is making now.

## Methodology and product implication

- The methodology remains the authoritative engine across all delivery modes.
- The application should distinguish an immediate question from a retained assessment or project.
- A useful chat answer may become the first record in a project without requiring the user to start again.
- The system should explain when its answer is partial and offer a proportionate route to deepen it.
- A deeper project should produce the full connected outputs required by the methodology and the consequence involved.

## Remaining boundary test

User preference alone may not be enough to determine depth. A person may request a quick answer to a high-consequence, irreversible or legally sensitive decision.

The next test should decide whether Operations Automated may answer provisionally while making the limitation clear, or whether it must withhold a recommendation and require a deeper assessment or specialist authority before advising action.

## Disposition

**Contextual convergence, with a high-consequence boundary test required.**

Draft the two delivery modes and shared minimum response as proposed methodology. Do not yet claim that a caveat makes every light-touch answer suitable for action.

## Retained lesson

Keep the method consistent and tailor its depth. Give the user a useful answer now, show its limits, and make deeper assessment a continuation rather than a restart.
