# Proportionate resilience and contextual challenge

## Source and boundary

- **Source:** Jamie Peppard's answer to the resilience-at-scale counter-test.
- **Scenario:** A hypothetical automated operation whose full throughput could not be recreated manually during a multi-day outage.
- **Permission to use:** Jamie continued the mutual challenge exercise and corrected the AI's interpretation.
- **Confidentiality boundary:** General methodology, regulated-sector comparison and hypothetical operations only.

## Correction to the AI challenge

Jamie had been speaking generally about a large operational design. The earlier AI response interpreted phrases such as service feedback and human recovery too literally, even though Jamie's answer already assumed:

- Defined success outcomes and measures
- Layered assurance and controls
- Proportionate resilience
- Business ownership of risk and continuity decisions

The useful challenge was recovery at automated scale. The less useful part was treating conversational shorthand as a universal claim that complaints prove success or that people must recreate all automated throughput manually.

Future challenge should first present the strongest reasonable contextual meaning of Jamie's answer. It should distinguish:

- A general example from a universal rule
- An imprecise phrase from the underlying operational intent
- A genuine disagreement from different levels of detail

Where the distinction could materially affect the conclusion, AI should ask or state its interpretation before challenging it.

## Contextual convergence

Operations Automated should define resilience proportionately around the business service and its consequences.

Jamie confirmed that:

- Minimum critical outcomes during disruption matter more than recreating the complete normal operation.
- Management and relevant stakeholders define service criticality, risk outcomes, impact tolerance and acceptable degradation.
- A critical service should not depend on one solution without a credible alternative or recovery route.
- Alternatives may include a separate system, different technology or AI, another service provider, manual priority processing or another operational route.
- Time matters: disruption may be tolerable initially and become critical or require crisis management after a defined period.
- Regulated organisations may have explicit operational-resilience requirements that do not apply in the same form to every organisation.
- Business continuity management or operational resilience should own or supply these decisions where that capability exists.
- Operations Automated should not prescribe a universal fallback-capacity gap metric.

This aligns with the FCA approach for firms in scope: identify important business services, set impact tolerances, map dependencies and test severe but plausible disruption. The regulatory rule is not imported universally, but its reasoning can inform proportionate operational design.

## Methodology boundary

Operations Automated should:

- Discover whether business-service criticality, minimum outcomes and tolerances already exist
- Reuse applicable business continuity, operational resilience, risk and crisis-management decisions
- Map how the proposed operation depends on people, technology, information, facilities and third parties
- Test whether the automation can remain within the defined tolerance or enter an acceptable degraded mode
- Identify missing ownership, evidence or recovery design as a readiness gap
- Avoid duplicating or overruling specialist, legal or regulatory frameworks

It should not assume that a separate business continuity function is mature, available or correct merely because management refers to it.

## Remaining challenge

If an organisation has no credible business continuity or operational-resilience capability, simply deferring to that function leaves the methodology without the inputs needed to assess readiness.

The next transfer test should examine a smaller, non-financial organisation whose management has not defined service criticality or tested its claimed fallback.

## Disposition

**Contextual convergence, with a transfer test required.**

Do not propose a universal resilience measure. Retain a candidate readiness rule: where automation could materially affect service continuity, undefined minimum outcomes, ownership, tolerances or recovery evidence should be exposed as a gap rather than silently assumed.

## Retained lesson

The business defines what must continue and for how long, within applicable obligations. Operations Automated makes that definition visible, connects it to the proposed design and tests whether the operation can actually sustain it.
