# Make every material decision and open work item actionable

## Source and boundary

- **Source:** Jamie Peppard's direct feedback after using the proposed Operate inbox.
- **Conversation reference:** Jamie observed that the inbox and its options did not appear to make anything progress, then clarified that every decision still owed by him should enter the system as a task or work item with a working action.
- **Permission and boundary:** authorised Operations Automated product and methodology feedback using non-confidential project material.
- **Authority at intake:** Jamie authorised preparation of working actions as Operate is built. Methodology meaning, product approval, release, merge, publication, external connections, spending and delegated authority remain separate decisions.

## Operational insight

An unresolved material decision, review, approval or follow-up is work. It should not be left as explanatory text, a passive badge or an implied future conversation. It should enter the same attention system as a durable item that shows:

- what is being decided or done;
- why it matters and what evidence supports it;
- who owns the action and who holds authority;
- the valid action available now;
- what happens after that action;
- what remains blocked or unapproved; and
- the retained outcome and activity history.

This applies to work generated while building Operations Automated as well as work entered manually through the product.

## Methodology feedback

Jamie judged that the previous Operate increment felt disconnected from the basis of the methodology. It classified, prioritised and connected work but did not consistently return the governed next action required by the approved output contract.

The approved methodology already states that:

- useful analysis and a governed next action must be returned as work develops;
- the product should lead the user to the current decision;
- it should explain what happens after approval or transition;
- material decisions require retained evidence; and
- routine low-consequence choices need not each become separate governance documents when the activity history is sufficient.

The signal therefore exposes a product-delivery failure and a clarification of how the approved method must be enacted. It does not currently justify changing approved methodology meaning.

## Strongest reasonable contextual meaning

"Every decision" is interpreted as every **material decision or action still owed by Jamie that affects value, risk, control, release, spending, authority or meaningful progress**. It is not interpreted as requiring a separate formal record for every reversible micro-choice.

The strongest practical rule is:

> An open item must not enter My Work unless it has a real current action, an accountable route and a retained outcome. Consequential actions remain explicitly human-controlled.

## Recorded evidence

- Live review of the 26 July Workbench showed an Approval in status `ready` whose detail panel offered only **Link related work**.
- The same live inbox showed that a Task could be marked done and a repository-backed release decision could route to the Decision Inbox.
- Source inspection confirmed that only Tasks had a direct Operate completion control, despite the Operations Bible defining states for twelve record types.
- The server accepted valid status changes, but the interface did not expose them and direct updates were not constrained to a governed transition route.
- This is founder-use evidence from one live local system. It is not independent-user validation of the final action vocabulary.

## Challenge tests

### Reverse test

If every conversational choice became a formal Decision or Approval, the inbox would become administrative noise and conceal the material work. Routine, low-consequence choices should therefore remain in the activity history unless a separate decision record adds useful evidence or control.

### Authority test

A visible button must not turn AI recommendation or technical readiness into permission. Approving an Approval, accepting Risk, authorising a Change and recording a material Decision require Jamie's exact confirmation and a retained reason during the founder-controlled phase.

### Failure test

An item with a status but no executable next action is not operationally usable. It should fail the action-contract test rather than appear complete because it renders correctly.

### Transfer test

Not every item should duplicate its source workflow. Existing methodology-change and Brand Review items may enter My Work as actionable items that route to their controlled source action. Native Operate records should progress through the Operate action route.

### Time-horizon test

Action names and transitions may need revision after real use. The contract should be explicit, tested and retained as proposed product behaviour rather than silently embedded in interface code.

## What changed the AI assessment

The earlier AI assessment treated the operational graph as the primary completed increment and lifecycle progression as later depth. Jamie's use demonstrates that this ordering was wrong: a graph that does not help the user decide or act is supporting infrastructure, not a sufficient product outcome.

The corrected priority is the complete action loop:

**capture -> understand -> connect -> recommend -> act -> verify -> retain learning**

## Disagreement and uncertainty

AI does not disagree with the operational substance. The only boundary is against literal over-formalisation of every small choice.

Remaining uncertainty includes:

- whether the first action wording matches Jamie's natural operating language;
- which transitions need stronger evidence or linked-work prerequisites;
- whether some Decisions should be generated automatically from other record states;
- how parent Case progress should be derived beyond preventing premature closure; and
- how the rule transfers beyond a founder-controlled, single-user environment.

## Disposition

**Clarification of approved method; material product correction proposed now.**

Do not alter the approved methodology in this increment. Add a governed action contract to Operate, require every open Operations Bible state to expose a working action, route specialist inbox items to their existing controlled workflows, prevent direct status jumps, retain transition evidence and verify real founder actions in the interface.

## Preparation outcome

The proposed correction now gives every open Operations Bible state a tested action, presents that action in My Work, retains its evidence and aftermath, blocks direct lifecycle bypass and protects Case closure. Approval and Decision journeys were completed against isolated temporary data through the interface; missing exact confirmation was rejected before the action reached the server. The full Workbench suite passed 72 tests with no failures.

This is technical and AI assurance evidence, not Jamie's approval of the action contract or release of the product.

## Outcome review trigger

Review after Jamie completes or rejects at least one Task, Approval, Decision and non-terminal operational transition through My Work. Revisit the action vocabulary, evidence requirements and Case progress rules from observed friction rather than assumed completeness.
