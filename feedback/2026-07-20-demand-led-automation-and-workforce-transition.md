# Demand-led automation and workforce transition

## Source and boundary

- **Source:** Jamie Peppard's response to the first manual founder methodology check-in.
- **Scenario:** A hypothetical organisation proposes replacing its entire first-line service desk with AI agents within twelve months.
- **Permission to use:** Jamie provided the response to test the founder challenge and feedback loop.
- **Confidentiality boundary:** Hypothetical scenario and general operational judgement only; no employer, client or third-party information.

## Jamie's operational judgement

The intended outcome should be reframed before accepting workforce replacement as the objective. Cost, accuracy, control and service quality may all be relevant, but the organisation must define which value it seeks and for whom.

The operation should then:

- Establish scope, demand and reliable measurement before selecting automation
- Make demand data sufficiently consistent to support prioritisation
- Break the service into functions, features and work types rather than attempt whole-service replacement
- Use an 80/20 or Pareto-style view to find high-demand opportunities that can create substantial value for proportionate effort
- Balance demand and effort with risk, controls, dependencies and service consequence
- Begin with bounded improvements and expand from measured learning
- Use experienced people to design, check, control and improve the new operation
- Develop people whose routine work is removed so they can contribute to higher-value work and the next improvements
- Treat workforce reduction, where pursued, as an explicit authorised business decision rather than disguising it as the methodology's objective

Jamie described a compounding effect: capacity released by one successful improvement can help the same people find, govern and deliver further improvements.

## What the approved methodology already covers

- [User-defined Value](../principles/user-defined-value.md) prevents cost, efficiency, automation or AI adoption from becoming an assumed universal outcome.
- [Operational lenses](../methodology/operational-lenses.md) cover demand, work types, data, people, capabilities, controls, dependencies and learning.
- The [readiness path](../methodology/readiness-path.md) requires stable outcomes, usable inputs, rules, exceptions, ownership, monitoring and recovery before automation or agentic delegation.
- [OPERATE](../methodology/operate-overview.md) supports staged prioritisation, redesign, testing and retained learning.

The first check-in therefore produced an operational answer broadly consistent with the approved method.

## Possible methodology gaps

The required reasoning exists, but it is distributed across several artefacts. The methodology does not yet provide:

1. A concise demand-led sequence from value definition and data confidence through work-type segmentation, prioritisation, pilot and expansion.
2. An explicit residual-work and capability plan when automation materially changes human workload or removes routine work.
3. Guidance for borrowing useful heuristics such as Pareto analysis without treating them as universal rules.
4. An explicit warning that high-volume work is not always the highest priority when low-volume work carries greater harm, obligation or systemic risk.

## Disposition

**More evidence, with a clarification candidate.**

No principle-level change is proposed from one scenario. Retain a candidate requirement for a proportionate **demand, residual-work and capability plan** when automation materially changes work.

Test the candidate against at least:

- One non-service operational process
- One low-volume but high-consequence process

If the requirement remains useful across those cases, prepare a separate methodology proposal and assurance pack. The proposal should distinguish:

- A transparent workforce decision made by an authorised organisation
- The methodology's obligation to expose human impact, retained knowledge, capability, resilience and alternatives
- A preferred redeployment and capability-building route without claiming that Operations Automated controls the organisation's final workforce decision

## Retained lesson

Start with value, demand and evidence; segment the work; select proportionate opportunities; develop people alongside the capability; and use measured gains to create the capacity for further improvement.
