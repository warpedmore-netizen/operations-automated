# Risk records and connected operational work

## Source and boundary

- **Source:** Jamie Peppard's answer to the rejected-risk authority test.
- **Scenario:** A hypothetical organisation declined a resilience control for a known single point of failure, after which the predicted failure occurred.
- **Permission to use:** Jamie asked AI to retain the learning and prepare an initial amalgamation of the founder challenge evidence.
- **Confidentiality boundary:** General methodology reasoning and hypothetical operations only.

## Strongest contextual interpretation

The central issue is not whether Operations Automated should create a special rule for a declined control. It is whether the organisation's ordinary risk-management system works.

When a credible risk is identified, it should have a retained record, an accountable owner and a governed route through assessment, treatment, acceptance, monitoring and review. Declining one proposed control does not remove the underlying risk. The risk remains open unless it is treated, transferred, formally accepted or closed under the organisation's applicable risk process.

Operations Automated should expose a missing or ineffective risk process, connect its work to the organisation's risk record and design for the residual eventuality. It should not pretend to become the organisation's risk owner or overrule an authorised decision.

## Connected work model

Jamie also described an outside-in way to connect operational work:

- A **case** retains the original person or organisation's need, interaction and overall history.
- The case may produce multiple **requests** and **incidents** that require different treatment.
- Repeated or systemic incidents and other evidence may produce a **problem** for cause investigation.
- A problem, dependency, change or other uncertain condition may produce or link to a **risk**.
- Controls, changes and resilience arrangements treat or prepare for the risk while records remain connected.

This is a useful relationship model rather than a mandatory universal hierarchy. Incidents may be detected without a customer case, risks may arise without a known problem, and terminology will differ between users and organisations. The methodology should preserve the connections and intended outcomes even where local record names differ.

## Decision records and proportionality

Jamie's principle is that decisions, especially those affecting risk or control, should leave a record regardless of organisational size.

The strongest proportionate interpretation is:

- Material decisions that change value priorities, risk exposure, controls, authority, resilience or consequential commitments require a retained decision record.
- The depth and format of the record may scale from a note linked to the work item to a formal risk or governance decision.
- Ordinary low-consequence operating choices may be retained in the case, work log or activity history rather than generating a separate governance record.

This prevents proportionality from becoming an excuse for having no evidence while avoiding a separate document for every routine action.

## Methodology implication

The initial methodology synthesis should include a connected operational-control model that:

1. Starts from the user, service or operational outcome rather than an internal process label.
2. Links cases, requests, incidents, problems, risks, controls, changes, decisions and learning where relevant.
3. Reuses an applicable organisational system of record instead of creating a duplicate register.
4. Creates a proportionate record where no credible record exists.
5. Keeps rejected treatments connected to the risk and tests how minimum outcomes will be sustained if the treatment is not implemented.
6. Identifies missing ownership, authority, escalation or review as an operational-control failure.

## Challenge result

The hypothetical failure was primarily a failure of risk management and ownership, not a missing funding rule in Operations Automated. The methodology should have made the open risk, rejected treatment and residual resilience route visible, but it could not accept the risk or force the organisation to fund the control.

## Remaining challenges

1. **Record threshold:** the boundary between a material retained decision and an ordinary work-log entry requires testing.
2. **Local terminology:** a mandatory case-parent hierarchy may not fit monitoring-led, internal or non-service operations.
3. **Risk ownership:** an organisation may name an owner without giving them the authority, information or time to manage the risk.
4. **Open risk without action:** a record can become ceremonial unless measures, triggers, escalation and review are used in practice.

## Disposition

**Contextual convergence and initial methodology amalgamation.**

Draft the connected operational-control model as proposed guidance. Retain the proportionality and terminology questions for later case validation rather than treating this response as approval of the module.

## Retained lesson

A rejected control is not a closed risk. Connect operational work to a living risk record, accountable ownership, residual resilience and a proportionate retained decision.
