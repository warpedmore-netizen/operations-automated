# Consolidate methodology, clarify AI accountability and restore a workable release baseline

## Jamie's direction

Jamie asked for the complete retained discussion, suggestions and feedback to be reconciled into an updated methodology; for the result to exist as a draft in GitHub and Confluence; for the outstanding pull-request work to be brought into one workable baseline; and for the Workbench route from feedback to methodology update and publication to work in practice.

In the preceding Workbench discussion, Jamie also clarified the intended accountability boundary:

- AI should challenge the user, do the analytical work and produce the best output it can.
- AI can be responsible for the quality and faithful execution of its assigned work.
- AI cannot currently hold human, organisational, legal or moral accountability for an operational outcome.
- A human with the required authority must review and approve consequential decisions and remains accountable for the result.

This is general methodology-design feedback. It contains no confidential employer, client or third-party information.

## Operational insight

The repository had accumulated useful but separated progress:

- v0.7 methodology architecture and reader guidance had been approved but remained on an open PR;
- a proposed Human-AI Collaboration amendment covered manual work, human-readable and AI-usable structure, early delivery-team involvement and capability transfer;
- Workbench feedback established plain-language main answers and informed actions;
- product branches implemented a unified operating surface, source continuity, governed action, Confluence Draft publication and Connected Governance work; and
- two older PRs contained either superseded implementation or unique retained pilot evidence.

The problem was therefore not a single missing principle. It was a release and coherence problem: approved, proposed, implemented and superseded material had to be distinguished and reassembled without treating technical integration as methodology approval.

## AI interpretation

The strongest reasonable interpretation is:

1. make v0.7 the accurate approved internal baseline;
2. create one proposed v0.8 methodology draft that brings the unresolved Human-AI Collaboration and accountability questions together;
3. keep product implementation, methodology meaning, Git merge, Confluence Draft publication and Live promotion as separate governed transitions;
4. preserve every open PR's useful evidence while preventing superseded code from displacing newer work; and
5. prove the Workbench and Confluence route end to end.

“Push live” is interpreted as integrating and publishing the authorised internal working state. It does not silently promote proposed methodology to approved or Live status, deploy an external customer product, approve spending, accept risk or authorise new connections.

## Challenge and boundary tests

### Reverse test

If AI were described as accountable merely because it performed most of the analysis or execution, an organisation could obscure who has the authority, standing and ability to accept consequence. The method therefore separates AI responsibility for assigned work from human accountability for authorised outcomes.

### Capability test

A human approval is not meaningful when the person lacks the information, time, authority or practical ability to challenge the recommendation. The draft requires decision-ready evidence and a real challenge route, not a ceremonial click.

### Automation test

Manual work is not failure or unfinished automation. Human judgement, empathy, tacit knowledge, physical presence and accountable authority may make manual delivery the better design.

### Product-boundary test

A working Workbench can improve capture, routing and publication, but it cannot decide methodology meaning. Product evidence can justify a proposal; Jamie's explicit decision remains the approval gate.

## Disposition

**Propose methodology change now.**

The approved v0.7 baseline is retained. A v0.8 proposal should:

- incorporate the pending Human-AI Collaboration amendment;
- state the responsibility/accountability boundary directly;
- make plain-language, progressive disclosure and informed actions a visible delivery rule;
- define the deliberate choice between manual work, AI assistance, automation and bounded agents;
- require early operational, product and technical collaboration when delivery feasibility matters;
- require capability transfer and usable first adoption; and
- keep receiver-centred feedback connected to governed evolution.
