# Methodology depth, operational coverage and reader structure

## Source and boundary

- **Source:** Jamie Peppard's review of the consolidated Operations Automated methodology.
- **Permission to use:** Jamie asked for the methodology to be brought up to date, broadened and structured so that its omissions and future development are visible.
- **Confidentiality boundary:** Methodology-design feedback only. No employer, client or third-party information is included.

## Signal

Jamie considers the existing content good but still too light to operate as a complete methodology.

The current architecture describes useful principles, lenses, readiness positions, the OPERATE cycle, output expectations and controlled evolution. It does not yet provide enough depth for a reader to understand how the methodology applies across the full range of operational capabilities, work types and organisational interfaces.

In particular:

- The operational lenses name many important considerations but do not yet form a comprehensive coverage catalogue.
- Detailed guidance is missing for many operational capabilities and work types.
- Linkages between operations and capabilities such as product, finance, people, legal, risk, compliance, technology, data, security, procurement, change and commercial management are not consistently exposed.
- The repository reflects the order in which the methodology developed more than the order in which a new reader would normally learn and apply it.
- Individual documents are useful, but the reader does not yet have a clear book-like route or page tree from foundations through application to specialist reference guidance.

Jamie's comparison with a framework such as ITIL concerns breadth, depth, consistency and navigability. It is not a request to copy ITIL, limit the methodology to IT service management or claim equivalence with an established standard.

## Strongest interpretation

Operations Automated now needs a guide architecture around its existing core.

That architecture should:

1. Give a new reader a numbered, progressive route through the methodology.
2. Catalogue the operational territory so that missing coverage is explicit.
3. Show material interfaces between operations and other organisational capabilities.
4. Apply one common structure to detailed guides.
5. Organise real use cases by scope, work type, consequence and intended outcome.
6. Record how deeply each area has been developed and validated.
7. Allow concise first use while making deeper reference guidance available when needed.

The immediate requirement is not to write a maximum-depth guide for every possible topic. It is to outline the whole territory honestly, identify priority gaps and then deepen guidance through evidence and use.

## Challenge and limitation

More documentation is not automatically a better methodology. A large undifferentiated manual could:

- Overwhelm readers who need a simple starting point
- Repeat the same principles across many pages
- Create false confidence that every operational context has been considered
- Import assumptions from IT or large-enterprise frameworks
- Become difficult to maintain as the methodology evolves
- Turn guidance into ceremonial paperwork rather than useful operational action

The response should therefore use progressive disclosure: a short reading spine, concise quick guides and optional detailed reference material. Coverage depth and governance approval must remain separate. A topic can be identified or outlined without being validated or approved for external use.

## Proposed disposition

Prepare Foundation v0.7 as a proposed guide-architecture change. It should introduce:

- A coverage catalogue
- A cross-functional interface model
- A numbered reader tree
- A use-case catalogue
- A common guide standard
- A visible coverage-and-depth scale
- An outside-in operating principle that begins with the primary customer, service user or stakeholder journey
- A minimum-structure rule that adopts useful practices without making any framework doctrine
- Receiver-centred feedback loops that are easy to use and progressively automated within human-controlled boundaries

Existing approved guidance should remain the controlled internal baseline until Jamie explicitly approves the proposal. No external publication, certification claim or application rebuild follows from this feedback.

## Follow-up signal: outside-in design, useful structure and feedback

On 2026-07-24 Jamie clarified that the methodology should begin from the experience of the primary customer, service user or stakeholder. The analysis should follow that person's trigger, request, click or journey to the intended outcome before deciding which operational people, work, information, controls, technology, suppliers and recovery capabilities must be injected into it.

Jamie also clarified that:

- some explicit structure is normally better than relying on invisible assumptions or individual memory;
- Operations Automated should adopt useful elements from established methods where they fit, without requiring framework compliance or copying a whole method;
- areas with the least understanding or control need a starting structure that can be developed and evolved;
- the method should expect its first design to be incomplete and keep improvement loops active;
- feedback should be easy for the person providing or receiving it, in a form and channel that work for them rather than for the convenience of the delivery team; and
- automation should reduce the effort of capturing, routing, synthesising and responding to feedback without acquiring authority over consequential change.

The intended outcome is to treat operations as a value-creating capability that improves products, services and stakeholder outcomes, not merely as unavoidable cost or administrative burden.

### Strongest reasonable boundary

“Customer first” should not mean “customer only”. The primary journey is the starting spine, but the method must expose effects on employees, other users, suppliers, communities and future customers, together with legal, safety, ethical, resilience and human obligations.

“Structure is better than none” is not a defence of unnecessary process. A poor or excessive structure can create delay, conceal judgement and become ceremonial work. Operations Automated should therefore begin with the minimum explicit structure that makes purpose, work, authority, evidence, feedback and recovery understandable, then retain, adapt or remove it according to outcomes.

Low understanding or control creates the strongest development priority when it intersects with material value, consequence or dependency. Uncertainty alone does not justify maximum process.
