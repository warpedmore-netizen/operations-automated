# Learning through Failure

Failure is expected and used for learning. Avoiding every failure can delay evidence, hide weak assumptions and prevent useful improvement.

## Safe failure

Failure should be bounded, observable, proportionate and recoverable where possible. The permitted scope and consequence should reflect the value of the learning, the people affected and the applicable legal, safety, ethical and human obligations.

> Fail small enough to remain safe, fast enough to remain useful and openly enough that others can learn.

## Review

Reviews should not default to blame. They should examine the conditions, decisions, controls and assumptions that shaped the outcome while preserving individual accountability where it is genuinely relevant.

Learning must lead to a retained lesson, decision or improvement. The result should be recorded in a form that can inform future work and assigned to an owner where action is required.

> Failure without learning is not experimentation; it is repetition.
