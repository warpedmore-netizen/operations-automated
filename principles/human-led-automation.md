# Human-led automation, AI and agents

Technology may execute work, surface evidence, generate analysis and recommend action. People provide its purpose, context, judgement, empathy, authority, leadership and accountability.

The existence of a technical capability does not create permission to use it or transfer responsibility for its outcome.

## Delegation decision

Before delegating work to conventional automation, AI or an agent, establish:

- The purpose, beneficiary and desired outcome
- How often the work occurs and what effort, delay or risk it creates
- Whether the real operation and its dependencies are sufficiently understood
- Inputs, information quality and permitted access
- The frequency and consequence of normal variation and exceptions
- Which actions and decisions are rules and which require judgement
- The justified level of delegation: none, automation, AI assistance or bounded agentic action
- Required controls, evaluation, evidence and recovery routes
- Who owns the delivered outcome and can stop the capability

Use the [operational readiness path](../methodology/readiness-path.md) before expanding delegation.

## Prohibited shortcut

Do not automate unclear work merely to make its confusion happen faster, at greater scale or with less visible accountability.

Do not treat AI fluency or an agent's ability to complete a demonstration as evidence that it can operate safely in the real environment.

## Human involvement

Human involvement should be retained where:

- Consequences are material or difficult to reverse
- Context changes the correct decision
- Empathy and communication materially affect the outcome
- Evidence is incomplete, contradictory or vulnerable to manipulation
- Risk acceptance, legal authority or accountability is required
- The system moves outside its tested conditions or confidence boundary

Human involvement must be meaningful. A nominal approval step is not a control if the person lacks time, information, authority or a practical way to challenge the recommendation.

## Agentic boundary

An agent must have a specific goal and operating environment, an identity, explicit permitted and prohibited actions, least-privilege tools, information and memory boundaries, human approval points, observable activity, stop conditions and a recovery route.

An agent may not expand its own authority, create unapproved sub-delegations, spend, publish, contact people, accept risk or change controls unless that action is explicitly authorised within a governed boundary.

## Monitoring

Every material automation, AI capability or agent should have:

- A named owner
- Intended value and success measures
- Quality, harm and failure signals
- A tested recovery, fallback or rollback method
- Accessible audit evidence proportionate to consequence
- Review triggers for performance, value, permissions and changed conditions
- A route for affected people to question or escalate an outcome
