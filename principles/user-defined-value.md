# User-defined Value

OPERATE does not assume that cost, speed, efficiency or any other outcome is universally most important. The user defines the value proposition and value matrix at the beginning, before deciding what “better” means.

## Value matrix

The value matrix establishes:

- The desired outcome
- The beneficiary
- The forms of value
- The priorities between those forms of value
- The minimum acceptable outcomes
- The applicable constraints
- The authority to make decisions

The matrix should be explicit enough to guide prioritisation and redesign, while remaining proportionate to the work.

## Conflicting outcomes

Where outcomes conflict, OPERATE exposes the trade-off and gives the authorised human the choice. It must not conceal the consequence of one priority behind a single score or present a recommendation as an unavoidable answer.

If none of the available options is suitable, return to Examine or Redesign rather than forcing a false choice.

## Obligations

User-defined priorities do not remove applicable legal, safety, ethical or human obligations. These obligations remain constraints on what may be chosen and how value may be pursued.
