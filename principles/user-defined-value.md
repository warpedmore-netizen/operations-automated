# User-defined Value

Operations Automated does not assume that cost, speed, efficiency, automation, AI adoption or any other outcome is universally most important. The user defines the value proposition and value matrix at the beginning of the work, before deciding what better means.

## Value matrix

The value matrix establishes:

- The desired outcome
- The beneficiary
- The forms of value
- The priorities between those forms of value
- The minimum acceptable outcomes
- The applicable constraints
- The authority to make decisions

The matrix should be explicit enough to guide operational assessment, prioritisation, redesign and technology decisions while remaining proportionate to the work.

## Conflicting outcomes

Where outcomes conflict, the methodology exposes the trade-off and gives the authorised human the choice. It must not conceal the consequence of one priority behind a single score or present a recommendation as an unavoidable answer.

If none of the available options is suitable, return to Examine or Redesign when using OPERATE, or reconsider the scope and intended outcome rather than forcing a false choice.

## Obligations

User-defined priorities do not remove applicable legal, safety, ethical or human obligations. These obligations remain constraints on what may be chosen and how value may be pursued.

The wish to become automation-ready, AI-ready or agentic-ready is not a form of value by itself. Readiness is justified only where the resulting capability supports the defined value within these obligations.
