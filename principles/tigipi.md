# TIGIPI

**Think Idiot. Get Idiot. Prepare for Idiot.**

TIGIPI is an intentionally memorable human-factors heuristic. “Idiot” describes the temporary position of having insufficient knowledge, context, attention or capacity. It is not a judgement about a person's intelligence, dignity or value.

Teams may substitute “error”, “outsider” or “novice” where appropriate, while preserving the three-part intent.

## Think Idiot

Approach the work without assuming prior knowledge.

- What would be unclear to a newcomer?
- Which terms, steps or decisions depend on hidden knowledge?
- What are we treating as obvious merely because we designed it?

## Get Idiot

Test with someone who does not share the designer's context.

- Can they explain what they believe they are being asked to do?
- Where do they hesitate, guess or take an unintended route?
- What questions reveal ambiguity or missing information?

## Prepare for Idiot

Design for mistakes, distraction, pressure and unexpected behaviour.

- Can an error be detected and reversed?
- Does the system fail safely?
- Is help or escalation obvious?
- Does the user know when an action has succeeded or failed?
- What happens when instructions are followed literally but incorrectly?

## Ethical use

TIGIPI must never be used to ridicule users, employees or customers, dismiss accessibility needs, or blame an individual for a system that invited error.

If a capable person repeatedly makes the same mistake, investigate the system before criticising the person.
