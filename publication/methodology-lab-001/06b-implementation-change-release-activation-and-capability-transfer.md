# Implementation, change, release, activation and capability transfer

## Deliver an operating change, not an isolated output

Implementation converts the target design into a working operational arrangement. It includes policy, process, role, information, technology, supplier, control, behaviour and support changes. A completed document or deployed feature is only one part of the result.

## Organise delivery around outcomes

Create a delivery backlog that links each item to:

- intended outcome or uncertainty to reduce;
- affected journey and operational capability;
- acceptance and control conditions;
- dependency and owner;
- evidence needed for a decision; and
- release or transition state it enables.

Use small increments where practical, but ensure each increment is operationally meaningful. An increment that cannot be used, supported or recovered may create inventory rather than value.

## Co-design across disciplines

Bring users, operators, product, delivery, technical and material specialist functions together early. Their role is to shape the design and test it against real constraints. Clarify who recommends, decides, assures and accepts each result.

Keep a decision log for material changes to scope, requirement, control, architecture, supplier, benefit, release or residual exposure. Preserve the reason and evidence, not only the conclusion.

## Prepare the operation while building

Develop in parallel:

- future work and role design;
- policies, instructions and decision support;
- information definitions, migration and access;
- technology, integration, monitoring and support;
- supplier and service arrangements;
- control, assurance and evidence;
- capacity, scheduling and transition coverage;
- learning, communication and change support;
- incident, exception and recovery routes; and
- decommissioning of displaced work and assets.

Do not leave operational readiness until technical completion.

## Test for decisions and real use

Build a test strategy from consequence and uncertainty. Include:

- intended user and business outcome;
- representative users and work types;
- normal, peak and low-volume conditions;
- boundary, exceptional and adversarial inputs where relevant;
- information quality and unavailable dependencies;
- access, privacy, security, safety and control behaviour;
- manual review, override, appeal and escalation;
- performance, capacity and supplier response;
- monitoring, degraded mode, rollback and recovery; and
- operator competence and support readiness.

Record defects, limitations and residual exposure with their decision owners. A successful scripted demonstration is not enough for consequential release.

## Make the release decision explicit

The release recommendation should state:

- scope and version being released;
- acceptance evidence and unmet conditions;
- affected users, operation and dependencies;
- residual uncertainty and exposure;
- required approvals and authority;
- monitoring period, thresholds and review;
- feature limits, staged rollout or restricted use;
- stop, rollback and recovery conditions; and
- communication and support readiness.

Separate technical deployment authority from operational acceptance, policy approval and risk acceptance.

## Activate the change

Activation means the intended receiver can reach the change, understand it and complete the first useful action. Prepare access, data, workload, support, communication and local ownership. Observe actual use and correct avoidable friction.

Track adoption in context. Non-use may reveal missing relevance, capacity, trust, access, capability or control. Do not label it resistance without evidence.

## Transfer capability

Define the capabilities required to operate, inspect, challenge, maintain, recover and improve the arrangement. Use practical learning, supported application, examples, rehearsal and access to help—not only one-way training.

Transfer is complete when the receiving operation can:

- perform the changed work under realistic conditions;
- understand important purpose, limits and dependencies;
- recognise and respond to failure or drift;
- exercise human decision and stop rights;
- maintain relevant data, rules, access and suppliers;
- recover or escalate; and
- improve without unnecessary dependence on the original team.

## Close and transition responsibly

Confirm operational ownership, support, funding, measures, review rhythm, documentation, open risks, backlog, supplier responsibility and retirement activity. Retain evidence and unresolved conditions. Do not close delivery merely because the project date has passed.

## Outputs and gate

Produce an outcome-linked delivery plan, working increment, decision log, test and assurance evidence, release record, operational-readiness plan, first-use and adoption evidence, capability-transfer acceptance, recovery arrangements and transition-to-operation record.

Completion requires usable operation. If the receiver cannot use or sustain the change, continue activation or state the blocker and recovery plan explicitly.
