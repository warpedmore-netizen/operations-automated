# Technology, assets, architecture and suppliers

## Technology serves the operating design

Technology and physical assets enable, constrain and shape work. They should be designed as part of the operating system, not selected before purpose, journey and work are understood.

This chapter sets operational questions and boundaries. Architecture, engineering, cybersecurity, procurement, asset, service-management and commercial decisions remain with their relevant authorities.

## Understand the current estate

For material systems, integrations, devices, equipment, facilities and supplier services, record:

- operational purpose and critical outcomes supported;
- owner, operator, maintainer and decision authority;
- users, volumes, performance and availability needs;
- data and information handled;
- upstream, downstream and shared dependencies;
- access, security, privacy, safety and control conditions;
- support, maintenance, lifecycle and obsolescence;
- known failure modes, incidents, manual workarounds and recovery;
- capacity, scalability and change constraints;
- cost and contractual commitments; and
- replacement, exit and decommissioning conditions.

Focus on dependencies that could change the operational decision. A complete asset inventory may exist elsewhere.

## Move from need to requirement

Translate the target outcome and operating design into requirements that describe:

- user and operational need;
- decision, information and workflow behaviour;
- performance, volume, latency and availability;
- usability, accessibility and supported channels;
- security, privacy, safety, record and compliance needs;
- observability, auditability and explainability where relevant;
- exception, degraded-mode, rollback and recovery behaviour;
- interoperability, data and integration conditions;
- maintainability, support and lifecycle; and
- acceptance evidence.

Avoid fixing a product or implementation before exploring options. Distinguish mandatory conditions from preferences.

## Assess options in the whole lifecycle

Compare reuse, configuration, integration, build, buy, manual design and no-change options. Include:

- operational fit and user value;
- technical feasibility and architectural coherence;
- information and control implications;
- implementation and migration complexity;
- people and capability needs;
- operating, support, licence and supplier costs;
- resilience, concentration and exit risk;
- time to learning and reversibility; and
- retirement of displaced technology or work.

Cheap acquisition can create expensive operation or lock-in. A technically elegant platform can still be wrong for the journey or organisational capability.

## Automation, AI and agents

Choose the work arrangement only after the work and consequence are understood.

- Use **AI assistance** where analysis or generation helps and a meaningful human decision remains central.
- Use **rules automation** where inputs and rules are reliable, exceptions are bounded and outcomes can be monitored and reversed.
- Use a **bounded agent** only with a specific goal, constrained environment, identity, least privilege, permitted and prohibited actions, approval points, observability, limits and recovery.
- Retain **manual work** where human contribution creates justified value or the operation is not ready.

For all arrangements define ownership, permitted information, change control, monitoring, exception handling, stop conditions and retirement.

## Suppliers and partners

Understand the operational interface, not only the contract:

- service and outcome expected;
- entry, acceptance and completion conditions;
- information and access exchanged;
- performance, control and evidence obligations;
- escalation, incident and change routes;
- subcontracting and concentration;
- continuity, portability and exit;
- ownership of data, configuration, knowledge and intellectual property; and
- capability retained inside the organisation.

Engage commercial and legal specialists before commitments become difficult to change.

## Release and operability

Before release, prove that the arrangement can be deployed, observed, supported, changed, stopped, recovered and retired. Ensure operational ownership, support capacity, access, runbooks, monitoring, alert response, backup or alternate route, supplier contact and lifecycle funding exist.

## Outputs and gate

Produce a technology-and-asset dependency view, outcome-based requirements, option assessment, target architecture and integration implications, non-functional and control conditions, supplier interface, operating and support model, recovery and exit plan, and acceptance evidence.

Do not select technology merely because it is available. Do not release an arrangement whose operation, ownership, limits, recovery or lifecycle are unfunded or unclear.
