# Target design, prioritisation, business case and roadmap

## Design from outcome to operating reality

A target operating model describes how the business will repeatedly create the intended outcome. It should be detailed enough to make delivery, authority, capability and exposure decisions without pretending every implementation choice is already known.

## Establish design criteria

Translate the evidence into explicit criteria:

- intended and minimum outcomes;
- user and organisational value;
- demand and variation to be handled;
- service, quality, access and recovery conditions;
- mandatory obligations and control needs;
- capacity, skill and knowledge requirements;
- information and technology constraints;
- resilience and lifecycle needs;
- time, funding and dependency boundaries; and
- assumptions the design must test.

Separate mandatory conditions from preferences. Record conflicts that require authority.

## Generate real options

Create alternatives that make different trade-offs. At minimum consider:

- improve the existing arrangement;
- simplify or remove work;
- change policy, demand shaping or service promise;
- reorganise work, decisions or capacity;
- provide better information or human assistance;
- configure, integrate, buy or build technology;
- use AI assistance, rules automation or a bounded agent;
- phase the outcome through transition states; and
- do nothing now while gathering defined evidence.

Avoid options that are merely cosmetic variants of the preferred solution.

## Describe the target model

Show how the target will work across:

- journey and demand;
- capabilities and outcomes;
- work, decisions, flow and exceptions;
- organisation, roles, authority and capacity;
- information, data and knowledge;
- technology, assets and supplier services;
- governance, risk, control, resilience and assurance;
- performance, benefit and learning; and
- transition from the current state.

Record work deliberately retained as manual and why. For automated or agentic work, specify permitted scope, input and output boundaries, identity, access, review, monitoring, stop and recovery.

## Compare and prioritise

Use evidence and judgement across:

- outcome contribution and affected users;
- obligation and consequence;
- size and distribution of benefit;
- feasibility and organisational readiness;
- dependencies and scarce capacity;
- time to value and time to learning;
- reversibility and exit;
- implementation and operating exposure;
- lifecycle cost and affordability; and
- opportunity cost and displaced work.

Scoring can support conversation but should not manufacture precision. Retain the reasoning and material disagreement.

## Build a proportionate case for change

The case should explain:

- the problem or opportunity and evidence;
- the intended outcome and beneficiary;
- options considered and why one is recommended;
- benefits as causal hypotheses, ranges and owners;
- implementation, transition, operation, support, control and exit costs;
- people, information, technology and supplier implications;
- material exposure, mitigations and residual uncertainty;
- dependencies, constraints and alternative use of capacity;
- decisions, approvals and assurance required; and
- stop, review and benefit-realisation conditions.

A small reversible change may need a one-page brief. A large or consequential commitment needs appropriate finance, risk, technical and specialist evidence.

## Create a roadmap that can be acted on

Organise the roadmap around outcomes and learning, not only deliverables. Show:

- transition states and usable increments;
- dependencies and critical enabling work;
- policy, data, capability, technology and supplier changes;
- decision and assurance gates;
- resource and capacity assumptions;
- measures and benefit checkpoints;
- release, activation and retirement activity;
- uncertainty-reducing experiments; and
- conditions that would replan or stop the work.

Do not hide competing initiatives that rely on the same people or platforms. Sequence should reflect constraint and consequence, not presentation convenience.

## Outputs and gate

Produce design criteria, target operating model, option comparison, work-allocation decision, proportionate case for change, benefit and exposure model, roadmap and decision record.

Proceed when the authorised decision maker understands the value, alternatives, commitment, uncertainty and conditions. Approval of the case should not be represented as approval of later releases or residual exposure unless the authority explicitly includes them.
