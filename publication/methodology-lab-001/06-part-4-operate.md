# OPERATE – the full improvement and implementation cycle

## What OPERATE is

OPERATE — Observe, Prioritise, Examine, Redesign, Automate, Test and Evolve — turns an authorised operational need into a tested and sustained intervention. It sits inside the complete Operations Automated method: the outcome, journey, connected operating system and authority should already be understood to the depth required by the consequence.

The stages are decision disciplines, not a rigid waterfall. A small improvement may move through them in hours. A cross-business change may run several linked cycles. New evidence can return the work to an earlier stage.

## Observe

Build a reliable current picture from the work itself.

### Activities

- confirm outcome, scope, affected journeys and authority;
- observe representative work and exceptions;
- gather demand, flow, quality, capacity, control and outcome evidence;
- compare documented and actual practice;
- identify affected people, dependencies and parallel changes;
- label evidence, judgement, inference and assumptions; and
- return an early useful finding where safe.

### Output and gate

Produce a bounded current-state evidence set, material gaps and initial hypotheses. Continue when the evidence is sufficient to choose what deserves deeper attention. Do not claim cause from surface correlation.

## Prioritise

Choose the problem or opportunity worth acting on now.

### Activities

- define value and minimum outcome;
- compare consequence, obligation, demand, frequency and affected stakeholders;
- assess feasibility, dependency, reversibility and time to learning;
- expose capacity and opportunity cost;
- identify the smallest useful intervention or learning step; and
- obtain the authority appropriate to the commitment.

### Output and gate

Produce ranked opportunities, rationale, trade-offs, success conditions, owner and next decision. A priority without displaced work, capacity or decision authority is an aspiration.

## Examine

Understand why the system behaves as it does and what conditions a solution must satisfy.

### Activities

- trace causes across journey, policy, work, people, information, technology and control;
- test competing explanations and contrary evidence;
- segment demand and variation;
- identify constraints, feedback loops and unintended incentives;
- assess exposure, readiness and specialist implications; and
- state what remains uncertain and how it could be tested.

### Output and gate

Produce a defensible problem definition, causal account, design requirements, uncertainties and no-go conditions. Do not progress a preferred solution when the evidence supports only a symptom.

## Redesign

Create a coherent future operating arrangement.

### Activities

- remove avoidable work and simplify policy, decision and flow;
- generate materially different options, including manual and no-change routes;
- co-design journey, work, roles, information, technology, controls, measures and recovery;
- involve operators, users, product, technical and specialist functions early;
- define transition states and capability needs;
- test boundary and failure cases; and
- compare options against value, feasibility, exposure and lifecycle sustainability.

### Output and gate

Produce the target design, option rationale, work allocation, transition and residual exposure. Continue only when the design is desirable, viable, feasible and governable enough for the next commitment.

## Automate

Make and implement the deliberate work-design choice. The name does not require automation.

### Activities

- choose deliberately manual, AI-assisted, rules-automated, bounded-agent or not-ready;
- define identity, access, permissions and prohibited actions where technology acts;
- implement in the smallest operationally meaningful increment;
- retain human review and exception paths that can change outcomes;
- configure monitoring, stop, rollback and recovery;
- update ownership, guidance and support; and
- keep delivery evidence connected to the original outcome.

### Output and gate

Produce a bounded working intervention with explicit ownership, controls and operating conditions. Technical completion without operational usability is not completion.

## Test

Create decision-relevant evidence, not ceremonial sign-off.

### Test dimensions

- intended value and minimum outcome;
- realistic user and operator usability;
- normal volume and performance;
- boundary inputs and unusual work types;
- missing, late, conflicting or poor information;
- exceptions, disputes, overrides and escalation;
- security, privacy, safety and control conditions;
- degraded dependency or supplier behaviour;
- monitoring, stop, rollback and recovery; and
- capability of the receiving operation.

### Output and gate

Produce acceptance evidence, defects and residual exposure, release conditions and a release, revise, learn or stop recommendation. Testing cannot prove absence of every failure; it should reduce the uncertainty relevant to the decision.

## Evolve

Sustain the outcome and learn from use.

### Activities

- observe adoption, outcome, variation, override, incident and recovery;
- compare results with the value and change hypotheses;
- correct defects and unintended consequences;
- maintain knowledge, controls, access, suppliers and technology;
- transfer capability and ownership;
- retire displaced work and obsolete assets; and
- retain feedback and route material methodology learning through governance.

### Output and gate

Produce retained outcomes, benefit and control evidence, capability transfer, next improvements and review triggers. A change is not complete while the operation remains unable to sustain or recover it.

## OPERATE record

Use the lightest record that preserves:

- outcome and boundary;
- evidence and working hypotheses;
- prioritisation and decision authority;
- target design and work-allocation rationale;
- implementation and control conditions;
- test and recovery evidence;
- release, activation and capability transfer; and
- outcomes, learning and next review.
