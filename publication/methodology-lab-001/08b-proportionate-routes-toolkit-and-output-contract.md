# Proportionate routes, toolkit and output contract

## Use the shortest responsible route

Proportionality means matching depth to the decision, not doing less thinking. Increase depth when consequence, uncertainty, dependency, novelty, scale, reversibility, obligation or contested authority increases.

## Six application routes

### Quick operational answer

Use when the question is bounded and reversible. Frame the outcome, apply the material lenses, check whether evidence is sufficient, then give a provisional answer, uncertainty, recommendation and next action.

Minimum record: outcome, evidence boundary, recommendation, human control and next review trigger.

### Improvement diagnosis

Use for recurring performance or experience problems. Add the journey, demand segmentation, connected current state, causal hypotheses, exposure and prioritised experiment or action.

Minimum record: problem evidence, current flow, causes, affected stakeholders, intervention, measure and owner.

### Full operating-model assessment

Use for cross-functional, structural or consequential change. Apply the complete context, journey, capability, people, work, governance, information, technology, readiness and target-design route.

Minimum record: connected assessment, options, target model, business case, roadmap, authorities and assurance plan.

### Implementation and activation

Use for an authorised change. Apply OPERATE, delivery and operational-readiness planning, testing, release, activation, capability transfer and transition to normal operation.

Minimum record: working change, acceptance evidence, controls, release decision, first-use evidence, recovery and ownership.

### Governance or assurance decision

Use where an authorised person must approve, reject, condition, defer or accept exposure. Present evidence, obligations, alternatives, uncertainty, recommendation, residual exposure, conditions and review date.

Minimum record: named decision, authority, evidence, decision, conditions and retained reasoning.

### Incident and recovery

Use when harm or critical service failure requires urgent action. Stabilise, protect, establish authority, communicate, recover, investigate, correct and retain learning. Specialist and statutory routes take priority where applicable.

Minimum record: affected outcome, actions and decisions, current state, recovery evidence, unresolved exposure and follow-up.

## Core toolkit

Select tools because they answer a material question. Useful aids include:

- outcome-and-boundary brief;
- stakeholder, authority and accountability map;
- journey and demand map;
- operational value-system and capability map;
- work, decision, information and dependency flow;
- evidence, assumption and uncertainty register;
- readiness profile by work type;
- exposure, control and recovery view;
- option and trade-off comparison;
- target operating model;
- proportionate business case and roadmap;
- OPERATE record and outcome-linked backlog;
- test, release and operational-readiness record;
- capability-transfer and adoption plan;
- performance, benefit and improvement view; and
- feedback disposition and assurance pack.

Do not create every artefact for every case. Combine them when one view can support the decision. Split them when different authorities, audiences or lifecycles require separate records.

## Main output contract

Every significant response or deliverable should lead with:

1. **Current understanding** — the outcome, situation and boundary.
2. **Findings** — material evidence and the interpretation it supports.
3. **Uncertainty and trade-offs** — assumptions, limits, contrary evidence and affected stakeholders.
4. **Recommendation** — a clear provisional or final view and why.
5. **Next governed action** — the smallest useful step, owner and timing or condition.
6. **Human control** — the decision, authority, approval, stop or specialist action required.
7. **Retention** — what evidence, decision, feedback or learning will be recorded.

Technical trace, source paths, detailed calculations and control mechanics should be available where relevant, but not lead when they obscure the decision.

## Standard artefact quality

A usable artefact should state:

- purpose, audience, status, owner and boundary;
- when to use and when not to use it;
- inputs and evidence expectations;
- practical activity or decision method;
- output and completion condition;
- roles, authority and specialist interfaces;
- risks, controls, failure and recovery;
- measures and review triggers;
- tailoring by scale or context; and
- source and change trace.

The artefact's governance status and practice completeness are separate. A detailed proposed guide is still unapproved; an approved outline may still need depth before independent use.

## Answerability and escalation

Before acting, ask:

- Is there enough evidence for a useful answer?
- Would missing context materially change it?
- Is the consequence within the current authority?
- Is specialist judgement required?
- Can the action be monitored and reversed?
- What must the user understand before proceeding?

When evidence is incomplete, provide the strongest bounded answer it supports. Ask only for information that changes the conclusion. Stop when a missing authority, unacceptable consequence or specialist decision makes continued action illegitimate.

## Completion test

The chosen route is complete when it has created the decision or operational result it was selected to create, the receiver can use it, material authority and exposure are resolved or explicit, and the next review or learning route is owned. More pages are not completion; usable governed action is.
