# Governance, authority, risk, control and resilience

## Governance enables legitimate action

Operational governance makes purpose, authority, decisions, obligations, evidence, control and review visible. It should enable responsible action, not merely add approval steps.

This methodology helps connect governance to operational work. It does not replace legal advice, risk ownership, regulatory interpretation, safety engineering, security governance, audit independence or formal organisational authority.

## Define decision rights

For each consequential decision, state:

- the decision and its boundary;
- who recommends and provides evidence;
- who must advise or assure;
- who can approve, reject or condition it;
- who implements and operates it;
- who may pause, override or stop;
- who accepts residual exposure where authorised; and
- who remains accountable for the outcome.

Separate permission to investigate, prototype, test, release, spend, access data, change policy and accept risk. Authority for one does not imply authority for the others.

## Understand obligations and exposure

Identify material sources of obligation and potential harm:

- law, regulation, contract, policy and professional standards;
- safety, security, privacy, fairness and accessibility;
- financial, commercial and service commitments;
- people, environmental and community effects;
- continuity, resilience and recovery requirements; and
- ethical commitments and stakeholder expectations.

Describe plausible events, causes, affected outcomes and existing safeguards. Avoid scoring precision unsupported by evidence. Escalate interpretation and acceptance to the relevant specialist or accountable authority.

## Design controls into work

A control should have:

- a purpose linked to an obligation or exposure;
- a defined trigger and point in the work;
- an owner and performer;
- evidence that it operated;
- criteria for success, exception and escalation;
- monitoring and review; and
- a response when the control itself fails.

Prefer controls that prevent or reveal harm near its cause while preserving usable flow. Multiple approvals do not automatically create stronger control. They may diffuse accountability, delay detection or become ceremonial.

Check whether a control is preventive, detective, corrective or recovery-oriented, and whether the set covers normal and exceptional conditions. Design manual and automated controls with the same discipline.

## Human review as a control

Human review is meaningful only when the reviewer receives decision-ready evidence, has suitable competence or advice, has enough time, possesses real authority and can change the outcome. For automated or AI-supported decisions, also define:

- what the system did and on what evidence;
- uncertainty, limitations and material alternatives;
- what the reviewer is expected to test;
- override, correction and appeal routes;
- monitoring for drift or repeated override; and
- what happens if no qualified reviewer is available.

Do not place nominal accountability on a reviewer who cannot understand or stop the system.

## Resilience and recovery

Assume that people, information, systems, suppliers, facilities and controls can fail. Identify critical outcomes and tolerable degradation. Design:

- early detection and clear incident ownership;
- safe or serviceable degraded modes;
- manual or alternative routes where justified;
- capacity and access for recovery;
- data, configuration and decision restoration;
- supplier and communication arrangements;
- escalation, public or customer response where needed;
- return-to-normal criteria; and
- post-event learning and remediation.

Recovery plans should be exercised at a depth proportionate to consequence. A document that cannot be used under realistic conditions is not recovery capability.

## Assurance

Assurance should answer a decision question with evidence independent enough for the consequence. It may include peer review, specialist review, testing, control evidence, audit or external certification. Record scope, evidence, limitations, findings, conditions and the authority making the decision.

Do not confuse evidence that a process was followed with evidence that the operation is effective.

## Outputs and gate

Produce a decision-and-authority map, obligation and exposure view, control design, human-review conditions, incident and recovery plan, assurance needs, residual uncertainties and review triggers.

Do not release a consequential arrangement without identified accountable authority, usable stop and recovery routes, and the specialist decisions required by the exposure. Do not allow risk language to conceal a value or authority conflict.
