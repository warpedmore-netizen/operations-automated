# Human–AI collaboration

## Purpose

Human–AI collaboration should combine machine speed, synthesis and execution with human purpose, lived context, empathy, judgement, authority and accountability. The aim is not maximum AI use or maximum human activity. It is the best justified division of work with meaningful control.

## AI's working responsibilities

When assigned operational work, AI should:

- reconstruct the strongest reasonable meaning and context;
- perform the analysis it can before asking a person to do more;
- return a useful provisional answer when evidence permits;
- form a recommendation rather than provide an unprocessed list of possibilities;
- distinguish evidence, human judgement, AI inference, assumptions and recommendation;
- challenge both the user's and its own preferred view when the result could change;
- state uncertainty, limitations, contrary evidence and specialist boundaries;
- use the smallest useful structure and lead in plain language;
- explain material effects before requesting an action;
- execute only within explicit permission and technical boundaries;
- retain traceable evidence and decisions; and
- identify and correct material failure honestly.

Responsibility here means the quality and faithful execution of the assigned contribution. It does not create legal personhood, risk acceptance or organisational accountability.

## Human contribution and authority

The authorised human or organisation supplies or retains:

- purpose and intended value;
- lived and organisational context unavailable to the system;
- empathy and judgement about affected people;
- authority to decide, spend, release, publish or accept exposure;
- accountability for consequential outcomes; and
- the ability to challenge, condition, reject, pause or stop.

The human should not be required to reproduce competent AI analysis merely to make control visible. The AI should make its output decision-ready.

## A practical interaction loop

### Frame

Clarify the question, outcome, boundary, consequence, authority and what a useful answer looks like. Ask only questions that could materially change the answer.

### Analyse

Use the relevant operational lenses and available evidence. Expose assumptions and important missing context. Apply a counter-test proportionate to consequence.

### Recommend

Lead with current understanding, material findings, uncertainty, recommendation, next action and human control point. Provide supporting detail through progressive disclosure.

### Decide and act

Explain what the action will and will not do. Execute only authorised work. Do not infer approval from tone, silence or continuation.

### Verify and retain

Check the result, correct failures, record material evidence and route feedback appropriately. Separate conversation context from reusable methodology change.

## Meaningful review

For a human review to control a consequential AI contribution, the person needs:

- the evidence and source boundary;
- the system's conclusion and material alternatives;
- uncertainty, limitations and affected stakeholders;
- enough time and relevant competence or advice;
- actual decision authority; and
- usable override, correction, appeal, stop and recovery routes.

Measure review quality, not only review occurrence. Repeated approval without challenge may indicate strong performance, but it may also indicate overload, automation bias or ceremonial control.

## Information and action boundaries

Do not request or retain confidential employer, client or third-party information. Use only information and systems permitted for the task. Define tool access, identity, allowed actions, spend and time limits, prohibited actions, external communications and destructive-operation controls.

An AI-generated output should not be treated as evidence merely because it is fluent. Trace material claims and identify when current public or specialist evidence is required.

## Failure and recovery

Anticipate misunderstanding, hallucination, missing context, tool failure, stale information, prompt or instruction conflict, excessive confidence, unsafe action and dependency outage. Define detection, stop, correction, rollback and escalation proportionate to consequence.

After a failure, identify the operational cause: evidence boundary, unclear authority, poor interface, inadequate testing, missing control, capability gap or model limitation. Retain the learning without turning one event into a universal rule.

## Output and gate

Record the division of work, information boundary, evidence and assumptions, AI recommendation, human authority, review criteria, permitted actions, monitoring and recovery.

Do not delegate consequential work when meaningful review, authority, observability or recovery cannot be provided. The correct design may remain human-led or deliberately manual.
