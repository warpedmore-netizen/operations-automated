# Information, data, knowledge and measurement

## Information enables work and judgement

Every operational action and decision depends on information. The important questions are not only whether data exists, but whether its meaning, origin, quality, permitted use, timing and limitations are fit for the decision.

This chapter identifies operational needs. Data governance, records, privacy, security, statistical and technical standards remain with the relevant accountable specialists.

## Map information through the work

For each material activity or decision, identify:

- information needed and why;
- source and method of collection;
- definition, format and relevant context;
- owner, steward and permitted users;
- quality, completeness, timeliness and confidence;
- transformations, calculations and model inferences;
- where it is stored, transmitted and retained;
- access, privacy, security and record requirements;
- how corrections and disputes are handled; and
- what happens when information is missing, late, conflicting or wrong.

Trace information across hand-offs. A field can be technically complete while semantically misunderstood by the receiving team.

## Distinguish data, information, evidence and knowledge

- **Data** is recorded representation.
- **Information** is data interpreted in context.
- **Evidence** is information used to support or challenge a claim or decision.
- **Knowledge** is retained understanding that enables action and judgement.

Do not treat a dashboard as evidence without understanding definitions, collection, exclusions and decision relevance. Do not assume undocumented tacit knowledge is safely available or transferable.

## Information quality is use-specific

Assess quality against the consequence of use:

- accuracy and validity;
- completeness and coverage;
- timeliness and currency;
- consistency and definition;
- provenance and traceability;
- representativeness and bias;
- accessibility and intelligibility; and
- permitted, ethical and secure use.

A dataset may be adequate for broad service planning but unsafe for an individual consequential decision. State the fit and limitation rather than labelling data simply good or bad.

## Design knowledge into the operation

Identify knowledge required for standard work, exceptions, judgement and recovery. Decide what should be:

- embedded in workflow or rules;
- available as concise guidance or examples;
- retained through communities, coaching or expert access;
- tested through practice or simulation;
- protected because it is sensitive; and
- reviewed when conditions change.

For AI-supported work, record source boundaries, retrieval conditions, prompt or instruction ownership where material, validation expectations and correction routes. Generated fluency is not proof of knowledge or truth.

## Build a balanced measurement system

Measures should connect activity to decisions and outcomes. Use a proportionate combination of:

- outcome and minimum-outcome attainment;
- user experience, effort, access and recovery;
- demand, variation and failure demand;
- flow, delay, work-in-progress and completion;
- quality, error, rework and exceptions;
- capacity, workload and cost;
- control, safety, security, privacy and compliance;
- adoption, use and override;
- resilience, recovery and continuity; and
- learning, improvement and capability.

For each measure define purpose, owner, calculation, source, frequency, segmentation, limitations, threshold or interpretation and the action it may trigger. Check whether targets encourage gaming, conceal tails or shift failure elsewhere.

## Evidence discipline

Separate recorded evidence, human judgement, AI inference, assumptions and recommendations. Preserve enough trace to reproduce or challenge material conclusions. Use qualitative evidence when it illuminates context or exclusion, but do not generalise one story without justification. Use quantitative evidence carefully; precision does not remove bias or causal uncertainty.

## Outputs and gate

Produce an information-needs map, critical definitions, source and provenance view, quality and permitted-use assessment, knowledge and retention plan, balanced measure set and evidence limitations.

Do not automate or delegate a consequential decision when the required information, meaning, permission or correction route is materially unreliable. Do not delay a useful provisional answer merely because perfect data is unavailable; state what the current evidence supports and what would change the conclusion.
