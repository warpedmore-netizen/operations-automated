# The connected operating model and capability coverage

## See the operation as a system

The operating model is the arrangement through which the business repeatedly turns demand and resources into outcomes. It includes more than an organisation chart or process map. It connects purpose, journeys, capabilities, work, people, decisions, information, technology, assets, suppliers, controls, performance and learning.

The aim of current-state analysis is not exhaustive documentation. It is to build a sufficiently connected explanation of how the operation behaves and why.

## Start with outcomes and capabilities

Capabilities describe what the operation must be able to do, independent of a particular team or system. Work from the intended outcome back to the required abilities:

- sense and shape demand;
- accept, classify and prioritise work;
- make or support decisions;
- perform and coordinate work;
- manage exceptions, disputes and recovery;
- provide information, access and assurance;
- monitor performance and control;
- maintain people, knowledge, assets and technology; and
- learn, adapt and retire obsolete work.

Map capability only to the depth needed for the decision. Record which capabilities are critical, differentiating, shared, weak, constrained, externally dependent or undergoing change.

## Connect eight operating views

### Purpose and outcome

Clarify the promise, policy, objective, minimum outcome and accountable owner. Check whether local measures align with the intended result.

### Demand and value

Show sources, types, volumes, variation, failure demand, service expectations and demand-shaping mechanisms. Check which demand is invisible or displaced.

### Work and decisions

Map significant activities, queues, hand-offs, rework, dependencies, exceptions and decisions. Distinguish elapsed time from active work and routine rules from contextual judgement.

### People and organisation

Understand roles, authority, capacity, skill, workload, incentives, wellbeing, tacit knowledge, collaboration and change history. Include contingent workers and partners where material.

### Information and knowledge

Trace what is needed to act or decide, where it comes from, how its meaning is defined, who may use it, how quality is known, and what learning must be retained.

### Technology, assets and suppliers

Identify systems, integrations, tools, physical assets, service providers, ownership, lifecycle, failure modes, constraints and exit dependencies.

### Governance, control and resilience

Connect obligations, decision rights, risk, controls, security, privacy, safety, assurance, escalation, continuity and recovery. Look for control that exists on paper but not in the work.

### Performance and improvement

Use outcome, flow, quality, capacity, cost, control, adoption, resilience and learning evidence. Understand review rhythms and whether improvement action is owned and completed.

## Map interfaces, not just components

Many operational problems live between components. Trace:

- what each party provides and receives;
- entry, acceptance and completion conditions;
- required information and its quality;
- decision and escalation rights;
- service, control and recovery expectations;
- measures and feedback; and
- what happens when the interface fails.

A hand-off is not automatically waste. It may be necessary for specialist judgement, independence or safety. The question is whether it adds justified value and remains intelligible and controlled.

## Diagnose through patterns

Look for system patterns rather than isolated defects:

- demand exceeds usable capacity because priorities continually interrupt planned work;
- local utilisation creates longer queues and end-to-end delay;
- poor information creates checking, rework and defensive control;
- policy or incentive creates failure demand;
- specialist approval enters too late and forces redesign;
- automation accelerates one activity but increases exception handling elsewhere;
- tacit knowledge hides a resilience dependency;
- measures reward output while value, quality or recovery degrades; or
- multiple changes compete for the same people, data or platform.

## Outputs

Produce a connected operating-model view containing:

- outcome-to-capability map;
- material journey and operational flows;
- work, decision and exception patterns;
- people, information, technology, asset and supplier dependencies;
- governance, control and recovery view;
- baseline performance and evidence gaps;
- cross-functional interfaces and owners; and
- system hypotheses to examine before redesign.

## Sufficiency test

The view is sufficient when it explains the important outcome and variation, shows where intervention could create or displace value, and identifies the authority and evidence needed for a decision. Deepen a component when consequence, uncertainty or dependency makes the missing detail material.
