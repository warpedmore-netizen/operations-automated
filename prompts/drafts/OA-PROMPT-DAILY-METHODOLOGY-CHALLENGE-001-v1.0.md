# Daily methodology challenge prompt

## Exact generated template

The Workbench resolves the three bracketed controls deterministically from the challenge date before sending this prompt. The controlled lists are versioned with the implementation. Changing a list or the template requires a new prompt version.

> Prepare today's 10-minute Operations Automated methodology challenge inside this Workbench.
>
> First use the RETAINED DAILY CHALLENGE MEMORY supplied with the request to perform a silent novelty and coverage check. Reject any candidate that merely changes the organisation, names or numbers around a conclusion Jamie has already given. Do not reopen a settled or deferred point without materially new evidence, a different affected stakeholder, a genuine transfer failure or a named review trigger.
>
> Today's controlled-variety starting point is:
>
> - Methodology territory: **[territory label]**. **[territory instruction]**
> - Challenge mode: **[challenge-mode label]**. **[challenge-mode instruction]**
> - Response artefact: **[artefact label]**. **[artefact instruction]**
>
> Use those three choices unless the retained evidence shows that the territory would be low-value or repetitive today. You may override the territory for a materially more decision-relevant unresolved issue, but state the reason in one sentence and keep the challenge mode and artefact format where practicable. Variety is a breadth control, not a substitute for decision value.
>
> Use the artefact itself as the challenge rather than always returning the same scenario template. Keep it readable in this conversation using short prose, Markdown tables or a simple text workflow. Do not claim to attach an image or downloadable document. Make the subject specific to current approved gaps, retained founder feedback, live project or product tensions, or connected evidence actually supplied to the Workbench. If current public evidence is not connected, state that limitation briefly and do not invent a public signal.
>
> Give the strongest provisional Operations Automated response, the weakness most likely to change it and one primary plain-language question Jamie can answer in 10 minutes. Separate recorded evidence, Jamie's prior judgement, AI inference and assumptions where they are material. Explain that the answer becomes retained feedback rather than approval, and invite Jamie to identify what is wrong, missing, impractical or inconsistent. Do not give a questionnaire.

## Controlled axes

The implementation contains:

- 12 methodology territories spanning purpose, people, flow, decisions, risk, information, technology, AI, implementation, evolution, delivery and commercial/publication hypotheses;
- nine challenge modes: reverse, boundary, transfer, stakeholder, contrary evidence, time horizon, failure, authority and omission; and
- 12 response artefacts: case file, document critique, workflow, red-team finding, after-action review, stakeholder exchange, decision memo, audit observation, scorecard, journey critique, transfer comparison and assumption map.

The date provides controlled entropy, not authority. The model may override only the territory when retained evidence makes the scheduled territory materially less useful. It must not treat novelty as more important than decision value.

## Evidence and authority boundary

- Earlier methodology challenge conversations and structured learning states are compacted into the request; unrelated conversations are not added.
- Eligible earlier challenge replies are backfilled additively into one unapproved learning signal per conversation; the original messages are not changed.
- Existing approved Methodology remains normative. Proposed material and connected material remain evidence only.
- The Workbench does not have a general public-web research connection. It must state that limitation rather than invent topical evidence.
- A founder response is retained as an unapproved learning signal. It does not approve a prompt, methodology change, product change, merge, release or publication.
