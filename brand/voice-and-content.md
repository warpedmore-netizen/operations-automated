# Brand voice and content

## Voice

Operations Automated speaks like a capable operational partner: clear about the outcome, curious about reality, willing to form a view and honest about evidence, uncertainty and authority.

The voice is stable. Tone changes with context.

| Voice quality | How it sounds |
|---|---|
| Clear | “Here is what we know, what it means and what happens next.” |
| Practical | “Start with the smallest change that tests the assumption.” |
| Human | “Keep a person where context or consequence changes the right answer.” |
| Challenging | “Faster handling could increase failure demand if the cause remains.” |
| Grounded | “This is a recommendation based on the available evidence, not approval.” |
| Inclusive | “Use the method at the scale and depth your operation requires.” |

## Tone by situation

### First encounter

Open, direct and low-jargon. Lead with the user's problem and the practical outcome.

> See how the work connects, decide what should improve and automate only where the evidence supports it.

### Guidance

Calm, structured and action-led. Give the answer or useful provisional position before asking for more work.

### Application

Concise and explicit. Controls use verbs. Statuses state what happened. Errors explain recovery.

- Good: **Review evidence**
- Good: **Decision needed**
- Good: **Could not save. Your work is still here. Try again or export a copy.**
- Avoid: **Submit**
- Avoid: **Something went wrong**

### Consequential decision

Slower and more precise. Separate evidence, judgement, inference, recommendation and authority. State what remains unapproved.

### Failure or incident

Plain, non-defensive and recovery-led. Preserve accountability without reaching for blame.

> The publication stopped because the source changed after review. No pages were written. Review the new difference before deciding whether to continue.

### Challenge

Respect the strongest reasonable version of the position, then test the consequence that matters.

> Your principle works when the hand-off is reversible. What changes when a missed hand-off could cause material harm?

## Message order

For most operational communication:

1. **Outcome:** the answer, result or current position.
2. **Meaning:** why it matters and who it affects.
3. **Evidence:** what is recorded, inferred or assumed.
4. **Trade-off:** what could improve or become worse.
5. **Action:** the next useful step and its owner.
6. **Boundary:** the approval, uncertainty or recovery condition when material.

Do not force every small message into six labelled sections. Use the order proportionately.

## Vocabulary

### Prefer

- connected operation
- whole-system view
- user-defined value
- human-led
- justified automation
- evidence
- working assumption
- human decision point
- useful next action
- ready, not ready, or evidence needed
- bounded and recoverable
- owner
- learn and evolve

### Use carefully

- **Efficiency:** name the beneficiary and what must not deteriorate.
- **Automation:** state the work and decisions being delegated.
- **AI:** state what capability helps and what authority remains human.
- **Transformation:** name the actual change.
- **Maturity:** prefer a readiness profile with evidence.
- **Best practice:** name the source, context and limits.
- **TIGIPI:** explain the ethical meaning; never use it as a label for a person.

### Avoid

- frictionless
- fully autonomous
- AI-powered as a value claim
- revolutionise
- one-size-fits-all
- set and forget
- human in the loop when the human has no meaningful information or authority
- resources when referring to people
- eliminate headcount as an assumed outcome
- guaranteed compliant, safe or ready

## Naming

- Use **Operations Automated** in full on first reference.
- Use **OPERATE** only for the Observe, Prioritise, Examine, Redesign, Automate, Test and Evolve cycle.
- Do not use OPERATE as a short name for the whole methodology or company.
- Use title case for product names and sentence case for page, feature and document names.
- Use a slash relationship for an internal endorsed capability: **Operations Automated / Workbench**.
- Use a descriptive endorsement for a named product: **Connected Governance — by Operations Automated**.

## Headlines

A strong headline should express an operational truth, tension or result.

Good:

- **See the whole operation.**
- **Automation starts with understanding.**
- **Connect the work to the decision.**
- **Make progress without surrendering judgement.**
- **When the operation changes, the method learns.**

Avoid:

- **The future of intelligent operations**
- **AI-powered transformation for everyone**
- **Unlock unprecedented efficiency**
- **Reimagine what is possible**

## Calls to action

Use the action and expected outcome:

- **Describe the operation**
- **See the evidence**
- **Compare the options**
- **Review the proposed change**
- **Record the decision**
- **Start with one work type**

Avoid generic labels such as **Go**, **Submit**, **Learn more** or **Click here** when a specific verb is available.

## Status language

Status is part of the governance system, not decoration.

| Status | Meaning in content |
|---|---|
| Idea | Unassessed signal or thought |
| Draft | Being developed and not ready for formal decision |
| Proposed | Complete enough for formal review |
| Approved | Authorised within the stated scope |
| Published | Approved for the stated external audience |
| Superseded | Retained but no longer current |
| Rejected | Considered and deliberately not adopted |

Never imply that “built”, “live”, “merged”, “accepted”, “discussed” or “ready” means approved.

## Inclusive language

- Refer to people by their role only when the role is relevant.
- Avoid assumptions about organisation size, technical skill, seniority or access.
- Explain specialist terms at first use.
- Describe accessibility needs as design inputs, not exceptions.
- Do not use “simple” to imply that confusion is the user's fault.
- Use **they** when gender is unknown or irrelevant.
- Write dates unambiguously, for example **25 July 2026**.
- Use UK English.

## Evidence and claims

Distinguish:

- **Recorded evidence:** what a source or observation establishes.
- **User judgement:** what an authorised person values or decides.
- **AI inference:** a reasoned interpretation that may be wrong.
- **Assumption:** a condition being used without sufficient evidence.
- **Recommendation:** the proposed course of action.

Do not turn broad applicability into a universal performance claim. The methodology can be designed for varied users and scales while an individual offer, product or case remains deliberately bounded.
