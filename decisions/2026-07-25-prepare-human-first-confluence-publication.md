# Prepare a human-first Confluence publication model

## Decision

After reviewing the first private Confluence library, Jamie Peppard directed that the publication approach should be reconsidered from the reader's perspective.

The Methodology space should read like a coherent book and practical body of guidance. The Internal space should read like organisational documentation, including policies, frameworks, product and functionality explanations, user guides, procedures, decisions and learning.

Git may remain the technical and authoritative project memory. Confluence should become the human-facing delivery surface.

Jamie additionally directed that edits, comments and feedback on documents should become challenges to the methodology.

## Authority granted

This direction authorises:

- retention and analysis of the feedback;
- preparation of a human-first publication proposal and assurance pack;
- design of a governed document-interaction-to-challenge route;
- a separate branch and draft pull request; and
- recommendation of a bounded pilot and migration approach.

It does not authorise:

- approval of the proposed model;
- changes to approved methodology meaning;
- creation or approval of policy requirements;
- alteration, movement, deletion or replacement of the current Confluence pages;
- another Confluence write;
- automatic retrieval or monitoring of edits, comments or feedback;
- new Confluence permissions, author-data retention or notification channels;
- external publication; or
- automatic publication authority.

## Decision still required

Jamie must review the proposed reader architecture, controlled-record boundary, document-challenge route, pilot scope, trade-offs and migration controls before implementation is approved. Automatic document-interaction retrieval requires a later, separate connection and data decision.
