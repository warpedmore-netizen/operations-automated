## Evidence and challenge

- OA-RESEARCH-001 draws on public authoritative, research and practitioner evidence with recorded limitations.
- The approved readiness, lenses, OPERATE and output contract already contain the underlying meaning.
- The first case used the real internal methodology-development operation, four open GitHub proposals, the current local Workbench projection, the paused AI-owner automation and observed stop/recovery evidence.
- RP-01 exposed that current delegation is suitable for bounded preparation but not unattended consequential execution; cross-system runtime trace and technical gate enforcement remain incomplete.
- RP-02 exposed that accepted outcomes, founder effort and actual provider/lifecycle cost are not measured, so full cost per accepted outcome and a scale decision cannot be calculated.
- The Workbench reported all retained learning dealt with and zero human decisions outstanding while an unscoped cluster still carried a methodology-change-candidate disposition and requested a Jamie decision. This is separate product/process feedback rather than a core-method change.
- The strongest alternative is to use existing guidance informally. It has lower record burden but does not provide a repeatable assurance or unit-economics route.
- A core-method change is rejected before case evidence.
- A broader Workbench build remains rejected before repeatable user value is demonstrated; the bounded completion-semantics correction exposed by the case should be prepared and decided separately.

## Trade-offs and residual risk

The guides may reduce ambiguity and expose cost transfer, but they can become ceremonial, encourage sensitive logging or imply false cost precision. The first case reduced that risk by adding short first-use cards and allowing explicit unknowns. Residual risks remain: the author performed the case, Workbench completion semantics are contradictory, runtime evidence is fragmented, technical enforcement of consequential gates is unproved and external-user value is unknown. Jamie and applicable operational or specialist owners retain residual-risk authority.
