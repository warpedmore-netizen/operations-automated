# v0.7 methodology coverage and reader guide assurance pack

## Decision in one sentence

- **Decision Jamie is being asked to make:** Whether the proposed outside-in design rule, journey-to-operational-value-system sequence, adaptable minimum structure, receiver-centred feedback loop, operational coverage model, cross-functional interface model and numbered reader guide should become the v0.7 baseline for internal validation.
- **AI recommendation:** Approve with conditions after reviewing the draft pull request.
- **Assurance position:** Ready for founder review; practice-level content remains incomplete and must not be represented as externally validated or publishable.

## Trigger and founder intent

- **Trigger:** Jamie said the methodology's existing content is good but too light in use cases and practical documentation, does not consistently consider the full range of operational facets or links with non-operating teams, and is not organised in a natural reading order. Jamie then clarified that the method should begin from the customer, service user or primary stakeholder journey; move from the identified need into the operational value system; select and combine useful methods for the context; use helpful structure without framework doctrine; treat specialist functions as proportionate interfaces rather than subjects of whole-function governance; and make continuous feedback easy for the receiver and progressively automated.
- **Founder intent:** Build a methodology with the visible breadth and usable depth expected from a mature professional framework, while keeping it outside-in, adaptable, practical, proportionate, AI-ready and human-controlled.
- **AI inference:** The immediate requirement is not to speculate into dozens of supposedly finished guides. It is to establish the complete territory, make missing depth visible, provide a reader-first structure and define a consistent standard for developing detailed guides. Every use should start from the primary journey, move into the operation that must sustain the outcome and use the minimum suitable combination of structure and feedback needed to learn and improve.
- **Remaining uncertainty:** The order in which the outlined practice areas should be developed to usable and validated depth should be determined through Jamie's priorities and materially different cases.

## Evidence and challenge

- **Supporting evidence:** Complete repository review; Jamie's direct feedback; the existing self-assessment and priorities, which already describe detailed coverage as partial; and structural comparison with current official information from ITIL, COBIT, APQC, ISO 9001 and NIST AI RMF.
- **Source strength:** Jamie's feedback is authoritative for founder intent. Repository evidence is complete for the current controlled baseline. Framework sources are useful structural benchmarks.
- **Limitations:** No independent reader has tested the new navigation. The benchmark does not establish equivalence, compliance or market value. Most practice areas are only outlined in this proposal.
- **Strongest credible alternative:** Keep the repository flat and add detailed modules only when a case exposes a gap. This would minimise architecture work, but readers would still be unable to see the whole territory, navigate it reliably or distinguish an intentional boundary from an unnoticed omission.
- **Consequence of no change:** The methodology would continue to appear more complete than its practical guidance supports, and development could remain reactive and uneven.

## Trade-offs and residual risk

- **What improves:** Work begins from the person's actual journey and moves into the operation that must deliver it; methods are selected for the situation; supporting interfaces become visible without whole-function overreach; feedback is designed for the receiver; completeness is explicit; readers have a normal route; future authors have a repeatable quality standard; and missing depth becomes an actionable backlog.
- **What may become harder:** More structure creates maintenance work and could overwhelm readers if every page is treated as compulsory.
- **Control:** Progressive disclosure, four reader routes, short chapter indexes and one canonical source per rule prevent the guide becoming a single oppressive manual.
- **What remains unknown:** Which practice families create the greatest user value first, whether the outside-in language transfers across sectors, whether feedback routes work for different receivers and whether independent users can navigate without facilitation.
- **Who can accept the residual risk:** Jamie Peppard for the founder-controlled internal-validation scope.

## Proposed validation conditions

If approved for internal validation:

1. Ask an independent reader to locate guidance for a quick question, a full assessment, an implementation and a governance review.
2. Apply the coverage map to at least two materially different cases, including one outside a conventional service-management setting.
3. Develop the first detailed practice guide using the authoring standard and test whether it produces a usable output.
4. Review the coverage catalogue after each case and record newly exposed omissions or unnecessary complexity.
5. Test whether a user can provide or receive feedback in a suitable form or channel, understand who owns the response and see what happens next.
6. Check that the primary journey has not hidden consequences for other affected people or applicable obligations.
7. Test the journey-to-operation sequence and the selected method or tool against an alternative approach.
8. Test one interface where a specialist control could become a delivery constraint, including proportionality, authority and secondary risk.
9. Keep external publication, certification claims, product deployment and commercial release subject to separate decisions.
