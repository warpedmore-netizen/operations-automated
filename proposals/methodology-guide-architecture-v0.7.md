# Methodology guide architecture, outside-in design and operational coverage v0.7 proposal

## Proposal status

This is a proposed change for internal validation. It does not replace the approved baseline, approve external publication, establish certification requirements or claim compliance with another methodology or standard.

Jamie Peppard retains approval authority.

## Proposed change

Develop Operations Automated as a reader-ordered guide system around its existing principles, operational lenses, readiness path, OPERATE cycle, output contract and evolution system.

The proposed architecture adds eight connected elements:

1. An outside-in starting principle based on the primary customer, service user or stakeholder journey
2. A journey-to-operational-value-system sequence
3. A comprehensive operational coverage catalogue
4. A cross-functional interface model
5. A numbered book and page tree
6. A structured use-case catalogue
7. A common standard for detailed guides
8. A visible coverage-and-depth scale

The purpose is to make the methodology understandable as a whole, reveal what is still missing and provide a controlled route from concise first use to deeper operational guidance.

## Reason

Jamie considers the existing methodology content useful but too light for the intended scope. The operational lenses describe broad considerations, but the methodology does not yet address the breadth and guide depth expected of a serious operational framework. It also does not consistently explain how operational decisions affect, depend on or require authority from non-operational capabilities.

The repository is governed and traceable, but it is not yet organised in the normal order a person would read and apply a methodology. A new reader should not need to understand the project's development history or GitHub structure before they can use it.

The retained feedback is recorded in [Methodology Depth, Operational Coverage and Reader Structure](../feedback/2026-07-23-methodology-depth-and-reader-structure.md) and [Move from the Primary Journey into the Operational Value System](../feedback/2026-07-24-journey-to-operational-value-system.md).

## Desired outcomes

The change should allow a reader to:

- Understand what Operations Automated is before encountering detailed tools
- Follow a normal reading route from purpose through application and assurance
- See the full intended operational scope in one catalogue
- Identify which guidance exists, how deep it is and what remains missing
- Understand material upstream, downstream and cross-functional interfaces
- Trace the primary person's journey before designing internal work and identify the operational injection points that support it
- Move from that journey into the operational value system, beginning with the first operational event and the outcomes that must be sustained
- Select and combine appropriate methods for the context without treating one framework as universally correct
- Select guidance by ordinary-language need or use case
- Receive a short usable route without losing access to deeper reference guidance
- Start with minimum useful structure, reuse practices that fit and evolve them through observed outcomes
- Give and receive feedback through a suitable form or channel with visible ownership and response
- Distinguish approved methodology, proposed material, tested guidance and examples

## Scope

The proposal covers:

- Information architecture for the methodology corpus
- A high-level catalogue of operational capabilities and interfaces
- Outside-in journey design and operational injection points
- The journey-to-operation sequence and explicit required outcomes
- Proportionate selection and combination of methods, including MoSCoW where useful
- Minimum useful structure and adopt-adapt-evolve guidance
- Receiver-centred, progressively automated feedback loops
- A consistent schema for future guides
- A catalogue structure for use cases and worked examples
- Navigation, numbering, cross-linking and visual coverage
- A development-depth model that is separate from governance status
- Validation of reader comprehension and guide usefulness

## Not in scope

This proposal does not:

- Rewrite or approve every detailed guide
- Adopt ITIL, COBIT, APQC, ISO 9001 or NIST AI RMF
- Reproduce proprietary framework content
- Claim compliance, equivalence, accreditation or certification
- Prescribe one operating model for every organisation
- Govern the whole professional practice of Finance, People, Legal, Risk or another specialist capability
- Make the primary customer or service user the only stakeholder or override applicable obligations
- Treat structure, automation or feedback volume as proof of value
- Require every small case to complete every guide
- Rebuild or deploy the application
- Approve external publication, pricing or commercial use
- Connect an external system or introduce new AI authority

## Outside-in design, adaptable structure and feedback

Operations Automated should begin with the primary customer, service user or stakeholder journey, not the existing organisation chart. It should follow the person's trigger, request, click, interaction or experience to the intended outcome and then identify the operational injection points needed to support it: people, work, information, decisions, controls, technology, suppliers, interfaces and recovery.

This starting point does not erase other affected parties or applicable legal, safety, ethical, resilience, accessibility and human obligations. A convenient customer experience is not a successful outcome if it creates hidden harm, unmanageable work or an operation that cannot recover.

Once the journey identifies why and where to improve, the method should move into the operational value system. It should start with the first operational event and follow the demand, work, decisions, information, outcomes, variation, failure and recovery from beginning to end. This is where a product or service promise becomes a repeatable outcome. Consistent operations sustains value and creates capacity for further change; weak operations can propagate harm, rework, cost, pressure, regulatory exposure and delayed product work.

The method should use the minimum explicit structure that makes purpose, work, ownership, evidence, authority, feedback and recovery understandable. It may borrow and combine useful practices from established approaches where they fit, but it should not require doctrinal or wholesale framework adoption. Journey mapping, Lean techniques, MoSCoW and deeper service, quality, risk or project practices are selectable tools, not competing identities. Their use should be explained, observed and adapted according to outcomes.

Operations Automated may improve the operational work inside any capability and the interface between that capability and an end-to-end outcome. It does not govern the whole of Finance, People, Legal, Risk or another specialist profession. Risk and assurance should help teams identify and treat material exposure early. A disproportionate or unexplained control that blocks a required outcome creates an interface problem and possible secondary risk to record and escalate, but delivery pressure does not remove obligations or transfer risk authority.

Feedback is part of the journey. It should be easy for the person giving or receiving it and delivered in a form and channel suitable for their context. Automation may reduce the effort of capture, acknowledgement, validation, routing, synthesis, reminders and reporting. AI may identify patterns and propose changes. Consequential judgement, authority and approval remain human-controlled.

## 1. Operational coverage catalogue

Create a controlled catalogue that describes the operational territory before attempting maximum-depth documentation.

The catalogue should group connected capabilities without treating organisational departments as fixed or universal.

### 1.1 Core value flow

- Purpose, stakeholder need and user-defined value
- Customer, user, employee and partner experience
- Product, service and portfolio lifecycles
- Demand, intake, classification and prioritisation
- End-to-end flow, fulfilment and service delivery
- Operational support, exceptions, escalation and recovery
- Cases, requests, incidents, problems, changes, complaints, audits and other work types
- Outcome delivery and benefit realisation

### 1.2 Enabling capabilities

- People, workforce capacity, competence, training and culture
- Organisation design, roles, authority and knowledge
- Information, data, records, evidence and privacy
- Technology, architecture, integration and technical debt
- Assets, configuration and resource management
- Finance, costing, budgeting and investment
- Procurement, suppliers, contracts and commercial management
- Facilities and other physical or environmental dependencies where relevant

### 1.3 Direction and assurance

- Strategy, policy, governance and decision authority
- Risk, controls, obligations, ethics and safety
- Security, fraud prevention and access management
- Quality, performance and service expectations
- Resilience, continuity, recovery and crisis response
- Compliance, assurance, audit and corrective action
- Measurement, evaluation and management review

### 1.4 Change and evolution

- Improvement, innovation and retained learning
- Portfolio, programme, project and change coordination
- Transition, release, deployment and adoption
- Process simplification and conventional automation
- AI assistance and AI risk management
- Bounded agentic operation
- Monitoring, intervention, rollback and retirement
- Methodology evolution

Each catalogue entry should have a stable identifier, purpose, intended outcomes, material interfaces, current depth, governance status and links to related guidance. The catalogue is an index of intended coverage; it is not proof that the guidance is complete.

## 2. Cross-functional interface model

Every material recommendation and detailed guide should identify the capabilities it supplies, consumes, constrains or changes.

The model should use capability language rather than assuming a particular organisation chart. Depending on context, one person may hold several responsibilities, or several teams may contribute to one capability.

The interface model should consider:

- Leadership and governance
- Product and service management
- Sales, marketing and customer relationships
- Finance and investment
- People, learning and organisation change
- Legal, risk, compliance and assurance
- Data, privacy and knowledge
- Security, technology and engineering
- Procurement, commercial management and suppliers
- Portfolio, programme, project and change delivery
- Facilities, physical operations and environmental dependencies
- Customers, partners, regulators and other external parties

For each material interface, record proportionately:

1. The value or outcome being exchanged
2. Inputs, outputs and evidence
3. Ownership, responsibility and decision authority
4. Timing, handover and service expectations
5. Dependencies and assumptions
6. Legal, safety, ethical, contractual or human obligations
7. Measures and unintended impacts
8. Failure, escalation, recovery and review routes
9. Change coordination and communication needs

This interface view should connect to the existing operational lenses and prevent local improvement from shifting cost, risk, delay or harm elsewhere.

## 4. Use-case catalogue

Create a catalogue that helps a user start from a real need rather than a framework term.

Classify each use case by:

- Unit of analysis: activity, personal workflow, process, journey, case, service, function, organisation or ecosystem
- Work type: planned work, request, case, incident, problem, change, complaint, audit, exception or recovery
- Scale and organisational context
- Consequence and reversibility
- Relevant operational capabilities and interfaces
- Current readiness and intended ambition
- Entry route: Ask, Assess, OPERATE, implementation or assurance
- Expected outputs and human decisions
- Evidence strength and validation status

Initial catalogue coverage should include materially different examples such as:

- Improving a recurring personal or team workflow
- Following a customer click or request through hidden operational work and back to the experienced outcome
- Reducing avoidable demand in a customer process
- Redesigning a cross-team service
- Managing a case that creates several requests or incidents
- Investigating recurring incidents and associated risk
- Responding to an audit or control weakness
- Introducing conventional automation
- Testing a bounded AI-assisted task
- Considering a bounded agent with access to tools
- Recovering a critical service after failure
- Closing a multi-channel feedback loop with visible ownership, response and retained learning

Every published example must be non-confidential, clearly marked as illustrative and linked to the version of the methodology used.

## 5. Common guide standard

Use the proposed [practice-guide authoring standard](../guide/10-tools-and-reference/guide-authoring-standard.md). Its twenty-section anatomy covers:

- status, applicability, purpose, readers, triggers, scope and terminology;
- value, beneficiaries, minimum outcomes, evidence, work types and workflow;
- decision rights, outputs, interfaces, people, information and knowledge;
- risks, controls, obligations, technology, measures, learning, failure and recovery; and
- proportional tailoring, implementation, worked examples and known gaps.

Each guide begins with a concise “use this guide when” summary and ends with a governed next action. The standard provides consistent coverage, not a requirement to show every user twenty pages. Detailed explanation, tools and specialist considerations follow through progressive disclosure.

## 6. Coverage-and-depth scale

Track development depth separately from approval status.

| Level | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Identified | The capability is named and its likely relevance is recorded. |
| 2 | Outlined | Purpose, boundaries, intended outcomes, main interfaces and material considerations are described. |
| 3 | Usable | A reader has practical workflow, roles, measures, controls, outputs and tools sufficient for proportionate application. |
| 4 | Validated | The guidance has produced useful outcomes in materially different cases and retained the resulting learning. |
| 5 | Publishable | The guidance has passed the required governance, assurance, accessibility and publication review. |

An artefact at depth level 3 may still have a governance status of proposed. A level 2 outline may be approved as a development map without its subject being validated. Both dimensions must remain visible.

For the initial v0.7 validation:

- All material operational areas should reach at least level 1.
- Priority capabilities and interfaces should reach at least level 2.
- Only evidence-led pilot areas should be expected to reach level 3 or 4.
- No area reaches level 5 without a separate external-publication decision.

A coverage heatmap should show depth, governance status and material dependencies without collapsing them into one score.

## Strongest alternative

Keep the current small set of methodology files and add only:

- One coverage index
- One navigation page
- A small number of worked examples

This alternative would be faster, cheaper to maintain and less likely to overwhelm readers. It may be sufficient if the immediate goal is facilitated internal use rather than a durable methodology product.

Its limitation is that an index alone would identify topics without establishing consistent guide depth, cross-functional interfaces, use-case navigation or a normal reading route. The proposal should therefore be implemented incrementally, and the alternative retained as the fallback if testing shows that the fuller guide architecture adds burden without improving comprehension or use.

## Risks and controls

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| The methodology becomes a large, oppressive manual. | Use a short reading spine, concise summaries, quick guides and optional reference depth. |
| A broad catalogue creates false completeness. | Display depth and evidence explicitly; do not equate an identified topic with usable guidance. |
| Existing content is duplicated or contradicted. | Map and reuse approved artefacts before drafting new material; maintain one source for each rule or principle. |
| Department names make the method unsuitable for individuals or small organisations. | Describe capabilities, responsibilities and authority; treat organisation structures as contextual implementations. |
| External framework content is copied or misrepresented. | Use official public sources only for structural comparison; paraphrase at high level; retain source links and an explicit no-compliance boundary. |
| Detailed guides become checklists that override judgement. | Preserve user-defined value, proportionality, evidence, uncertainty, obligations and authorised human decisions. |
| “Customer first” hides employee, supplier, community or future-customer harm. | Use the primary journey as the starting spine, then expose all materially affected parties and non-negotiable obligations. |
| “Some structure is better than none” creates unnecessary bureaucracy. | Begin with minimum useful structure, measure its effect and remove or adapt anything that adds effort without useful value, control or learning. |
| Feedback is designed for collection convenience rather than the receiver. | Test effort, accessibility, channel, acknowledgement, ownership, response and outcome with the intended person. |
| Automated feedback handling creates hidden authority or amplifies noise. | Separate capture and synthesis from approval; retain provenance, consequence-based prioritisation, human judgement and an appeal or correction route. |
| Navigation and identifiers become difficult to maintain. | Use a controlled guide manifest, stable identifiers, automated link checks and one generated contents tree. |
| The catalogue expands faster than validation. | Prioritise outlines and deepen only where use cases, risk or reader demand justify the effort. |
| AI drafts confident but unsupported guidance. | Distinguish source, inference and proposal; require specialist or authorised human review where consequence or professional obligation demands it. |

## Validation plan

### Phase 1: inventory and architecture

1. Map every current methodology artefact to the proposed guide tree.
2. Create the initial operational coverage register.
3. Assign depth and governance status independently.
4. Identify duplicates, missing links and terminology conflicts.
5. Produce a visual system map, reader journey and coverage heatmap.

### Phase 2: outline the whole territory

1. Bring every material catalogue area to at least level 1.
2. Outline the highest-priority operational capabilities and interfaces to level 2.
3. Record missing specialist evidence rather than inventing detail.
4. Review the catalogue with Jamie for scope, language and priority.

### Phase 3: test guide depth

Develop at least three materially different pilot guides:

- One everyday demand or work-management guide
- One consequential risk, control or resilience guide
- One cross-functional automation or AI guide

Each pilot should use the common guide standard and provide a concise route plus optional depth.

### Phase 4: reader and use-case validation

Test whether a person without repository knowledge can:

- Explain what the methodology is
- Find the correct route from an ordinary-language problem
- Begin from the primary person's journey and identify the supporting operational injection points
- Identify affected operational and non-operational capabilities
- Produce a useful output without completing irrelevant guidance
- Give or receive feedback through an understandable, proportionate route and see what happens next
- Locate deeper help, evidence requirements and human decisions

Record navigation time, misunderstood terms, unnecessary effort, missing guidance, output usefulness and requested support.

### Phase 5: assurance and decision

1. Check UK English, accessibility, links, identifiers, status and confidentiality.
2. Review source attribution and avoid proprietary reproduction.
3. Compare the resulting coverage with the official benchmark structures only to identify omissions.
4. Run at least two materially different, non-confidential cases.
5. Present retained evidence, unresolved gaps and the proposed release decision to Jamie.

## Human and AI responsibilities

AI may:

- Inventory and classify existing material
- Draft the tree, catalogue, outlines, cross-links and visualisations
- Identify gaps, duplication and inconsistent terminology
- Prepare use cases, guide drafts, tests and decision packets
- Run authorised repository checks and propose updates

Jamie or another authorised human:

- Defines the intended scope and value
- Confirms the primary journey, affected people and feedback needs
- Challenges the catalogue and priority order
- Confirms material organisational and human considerations
- Decides whether guide depth is useful and proportionate
- Approves consequential methodology meaning, governance status and release

Specialist review remains necessary where legal, regulatory, safety, security, financial, professional or sector-specific guidance could create material consequence.

## Recommendation

Approve the guide architecture for internal validation, not as a completed methodology.

Begin outside-in from the primary journey and intended outcome. Use the minimum structure required to make the supporting operation understandable, make feedback easy and owned, and expose the complete operational territory. Then deepen only the capabilities justified by use cases, consequence and evidence.
