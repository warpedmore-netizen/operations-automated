# Operational lenses

Operational lenses are connected views used to understand an operation as a whole before deciding what to improve or automate.

They apply to small personal workflows, team processes, services and wider operating models. Use them proportionately: select the lenses that can materially affect the outcome, but record why a consequential lens is considered out of scope.

## 1. Purpose, value and outcomes

Understand why the operation exists, who benefits and what better means.

Consider:

- Intended outcomes and minimum acceptable outcomes
- Beneficiaries, users and people who may be adversely affected
- Forms and priorities of value
- Conflicting outcomes and applicable obligations
- Who can decide when outcomes conflict

Expected output: an agreed scope and proportionate value matrix.

## 2. People and experience

Understand how the operation is experienced and performed by people.

Consider:

- Needs, accessibility, effort and communication
- Skills, capacity, workload and incentives
- Hidden knowledge, workarounds and individual heroics
- Where empathy, context or judgement changes the correct action
- Who is excluded, disadvantaged or placed at risk by the current design

Expected output: affected-person view, capability needs and human judgement points.

## 3. Demand and work types

Understand what enters the operation, how it varies and what treatment each type requires.

Consider:

- Sources, volumes, patterns, urgency and seasonality
- Cases, requests, incidents, problems, changes, audits, exceptions and planned work
- Entry criteria, classification, priority and service expectations
- Preventable, duplicate, failed or transferred demand
- Demand that should be removed, redirected or handled differently

Expected output: demand profile and work-type model.

## 4. Flow and dependencies

Understand how work, information and decisions move through the operation.

Consider:

- Activities, queues, waiting, rework and handovers
- Upstream and downstream teams, suppliers, systems and customers
- Dependencies, bottlenecks, single points of failure and feedback loops
- Normal, exceptional and recovery routes
- Differences between the documented process and real work

Expected output: current operational flow, dependency map and friction points.

## 5. Ownership, decisions and knowledge

Understand who owns outcomes and how decisions are made.

Consider:

- End-to-end accountability and local responsibility
- Decision rights, escalation and acceptance of risk
- Rules, judgement, discretion and unresolved ambiguity
- Knowledge sources, accuracy, access, maintenance and expiry
- What must be visible for another person or system to act safely

Expected output: ownership model, decision map and knowledge requirements.

## 6. Risk, controls and resilience

Understand what could cause harm or prevent recovery.

Consider:

- Legal, regulatory, safety, ethical, human and contractual obligations
- Privacy, security, fraud, quality and financial exposure
- Preventive, detective and corrective controls
- Failure signals, escalation, continuity, recovery and rollback
- Whether controls reduce risk in practice or create ceremonial work

Expected output: risk-and-control view with failure, recovery and review conditions.

## 7. Information, data and evidence

Understand what the operation knows and whether that knowledge is reliable enough for decisions.

Consider:

- Required inputs, provenance, quality, completeness and timeliness
- Definitions, identifiers, records and audit history
- Access, classification, retention, deletion and ownership
- Measures that show outcomes, flow, quality and unintended effects
- Missing evidence, uncertainty and assumptions

Expected output: information requirements, evidence gaps and measurement model.

## 8. Technology, automation, AI and agents

Understand how technology currently supports the operation and what level of delegation is justified.

Consider:

- Existing systems, integrations, constraints and technical debt
- Repeatable tasks and rule-based decisions suitable for conventional automation
- Language, prediction or synthesis tasks where AI may assist
- Goals, tools and bounded actions that an agent might perform
- Permissions, human checkpoints, monitoring, evaluation, recovery and ownership
- Credible non-technical alternatives

Expected output: technology and delegation decision with a human-control model.

## 9. Performance, change and learning

Understand whether the operation can detect outcomes, implement change and retain learning.

Consider:

- Outcome, service, flow, quality, risk and experience measures
- Baselines, targets, leading signals and unintended consequences
- Change capacity, adoption, training and communication
- Tests, experiments, reviews and retained lessons
- Triggers for intervention, redesign or another OPERATE cycle

Expected output: outcome measures, change route and learning loop.

## Connections between lenses

Analysis must expose material connections rather than optimise one lens in isolation. Each recommendation should state:

- Which lenses it affects
- What improves and for whom
- What may become harder or riskier
- Which dependencies and assumptions must be tested
- Who is authorised to accept the resulting trade-off

Specific modules for cases, requests, incidents, problems, changes, audits and other work types may extend these lenses after pilot evidence demonstrates where specialised guidance is useful.
