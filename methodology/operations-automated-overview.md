# Operations Automated methodology architecture

Operations Automated is a living, human-led system for understanding, governing and improving operations in a world that includes conventional technology, automation, artificial intelligence and agentic AI.

It is intended to help an individual, team or organisation move from an unclear or fragile operation towards one that is understandable, controlled, measurable, improvable and ready for the level of technology that creates justified value.

The methodology does not assume that automation or AI is always the answer. It helps the authorised human decide whether to simplify, standardise, redesign, automate, introduce AI, delegate bounded work to agents or deliberately retain human delivery.

## Important distinction

OPERATE is the improvement and implementation cycle inside Operations Automated. It is not the complete methodology.

| Component | Role in the system |
|---|---|
| Operations Automated methodology | The complete approach for understanding, managing, improving and evolving operations |
| Operational lenses | The connected views used to understand an operation as a whole |
| Readiness path | The evidence needed to move from operational basics towards safe automation, AI and agentic operation |
| OPERATE | The repeatable cycle used to investigate, decide, implement, test and retain improvement |
| Methodology evolution system | The AI-managed, human-controlled process that improves the methodology itself |
| Delivery system | The way a practitioner receives analysis, recommendations, working artefacts and governed next actions |

These components should be developed and tested together. Evidence from applying the methodology to real work, including Operations Automated itself, should feed its controlled evolution.

## Scope

The unit of analysis may be:

- An individual task or recurring activity
- A process, workflow or customer journey
- A case, request, incident, problem, change, audit or other work type
- A service, function or cross-team operating system
- A team, organisation or network of organisations

The same core reasoning applies at each level, but the depth of evidence, governance and testing should reflect the value, risk, complexity and consequence involved.

## Architecture

### 1. Direction and boundaries

Begin by establishing:

- Purpose and intended outcome
- Beneficiaries and people affected
- User-defined forms and priorities of value
- Scope and system boundaries
- Ownership and decision authority
- Legal, safety, ethical, human and organisational obligations

This foundation prevents speed, cost, efficiency, automation or AI adoption from becoming an assumed goal.

### 2. Operational understanding

Use the [operational lenses](operational-lenses.md) to understand how the operation works as a connected system. The lenses cover purpose, people, demand, work types, flow, dependencies, authority, risk, information, technology, performance and learning.

The lenses are not independent checklists. A change in one may alter several others. For example, automating a handover may change accountability, evidence, risk, customer experience and the work required from another team.

### 3. Readiness and ambition

Use the [operational readiness path](readiness-path.md) to identify what is already reliable, what is missing and what level of change is justified.

Readiness is a profile rather than a single maturity score. An operation may have strong technical infrastructure but weak ownership, or clear processes but unsuitable data. It may be ready for conventional automation in one area and require human delivery in another.

### 4. Improvement and implementation

Use [OPERATE](operate-overview.md) to move from observed reality to a tested and retained change. OPERATE can improve basic work, redesign an operating model, implement conventional automation, introduce AI assistance or establish a bounded agentic capability.

### 5. Delivery and useful outputs

Use the [methodology output contract](output-contract.md) to ensure the methodology returns value throughout the work. Questions and records support the analysis; they are not the product by themselves.

### 6. Controlled evolution

Use the [methodology evolution system](../evolution/methodology-evolution-system.md) to turn questions, feedback, operational outcomes, failures, research and contradictions into governed methodology proposals.

AI may manage collection, analysis, drafting, checking and notification. An authorised human retains control over meaning, approval, release and consequence.

## Minimum complete outcome

A proportionate application of Operations Automated should leave the user with:

- A clear operational scope and value definition
- An evidence-based picture of current work and its connections
- A readiness profile covering operational, automation, AI and agentic considerations where relevant
- Prioritised opportunities with visible reasoning and trade-offs
- A recommended target state and implementation route
- Defined human, automation, AI and agent responsibilities
- Risks, controls, tests, measures, recovery and review triggers
- Retained decisions and learning

Not every case needs a large document. A small, reversible change may produce a concise decision and test plan. A consequential cross-team or agentic change requires stronger evidence, named authority and a defensible record.

## Working boundaries

Operations Automated must not:

- Treat every operation as a candidate for automation or AI
- Present a framework score as proof that an operation is safe or effective
- Hide uncertainty, conflicting evidence or material trade-offs
- Replace applicable professional, legal, safety, security or ethical obligations
- Allow AI or agents to acquire authority merely because a technical capability exists
- Turn operational improvement into paperwork without a useful decision or change
