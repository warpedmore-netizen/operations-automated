# Cross-functional interface model

## Purpose and status

This proposed model helps a person, team or organisation make the connections between operational work and other capabilities explicit. It extends the [operational coverage model](operational-coverage-model.md), [operational lenses](operational-lenses.md) and [connected work, risk and control](connected-work-risk-and-control.md).

The interface names below describe capabilities and responsibilities, not a required organisation chart. Finance may be a specialist team, an owner-manager, a shared service or an external accountant. The same principle applies to legal, people, technology, risk and every other interface.

Operations Automated does not decide that an interface requires a department or committee. It requires the relevant purpose, exchange, authority, dependency and failure route to be understood proportionately.

## Why interfaces matter

An operation may appear locally successful while transferring cost, delay, risk or harm elsewhere. Common interface failures include:

- sales commitments that the operation cannot fulfil;
- product or technology change without operational acceptance or recovery;
- controls designed without the people or data needed to perform them;
- a supplier contract that omits the required outcome or exit route;
- workforce change without capability, consultation or resilience;
- customer feedback that never reaches problem or product decisions;
- legal, safety, privacy or financial authority being assumed rather than obtained; and
- several teams measuring their own activity while nobody owns the end-to-end outcome.

An interface is effective when the right evidence, decision and accountability cross the boundary in time for the outcome to be protected.

## Outside-in interface rule

Do not begin with a list of departments and ask how they hand work to one another. Begin with the primary customer, service user or stakeholder journey and the outcome it is meant to create. Identify each point where the journey needs an operational capability, decision, control, communication or recovery route, then define the interface that supports it.

This keeps the interface connected to end-to-end value. It also exposes when an internally convenient hand-off makes the experience slower, less understandable or less safe. The primary journey remains subject to the needs of other affected people and to applicable legal, safety, ethical, resilience and human obligations.

## When to define or review an interface

Review the relevant interfaces when:

- a new service, process, channel, product, supplier or technology is introduced;
- demand, priority, volume, risk, cost or user need materially changes;
- work or information crosses a person, team, organisation, system or location boundary;
- an approval, specialist interpretation or acceptance of consequence is required;
- a handover, queue, duplicate record or conflicting measure creates friction;
- automation, AI or an agent will use information or act across a boundary;
- an incident, complaint, audit, control failure or missed outcome exposes unclear ownership;
- a change affects roles, contracts, policies, data, access, facilities or external claims; or
- continuity depends on another capability, person, supplier or physical resource.

## Minimum interface contract

Define these elements proportionately for every material interface:

1. **Why:** the shared outcome, obligation, decision or dependency the interface protects.
2. **When:** the event, threshold, stage or condition that activates the interface.
3. **Inputs:** the evidence, request, context or decision supplied by the operation.
4. **Outputs:** the decision, capability, constraint, service or evidence returned to the operation.
5. **Authority:** who may advise, perform, approve, commit, accept risk, stop work or change the boundary.
6. **Accountability:** who retains ownership of the end-to-end operational outcome.
7. **Response expectation:** required timing, quality, format and status visibility.
8. **Escalation:** the failure, conflict, delay, threshold or harm that requires another route.
9. **Recovery:** what happens when the interface or dependency is unavailable.
10. **Evidence and review:** where the exchange and decision are retained and what should trigger reconsideration.

A handover is not complete because information was sent. Confirm that the receiver can understand, accept and act on it, or make rejection and recovery visible.

## Mapping method

### 1. Start from the person, journey and end-to-end outcome

Define the primary customer, service user, stakeholder or beneficiary; their trigger, interaction or journey; the intended and minimum outcomes; and the end-to-end owner. Avoid beginning with existing department boundaries.

### 2. Follow work, information, decisions and consequence

Map where each moves or changes. Include normal, exceptional, escalated and degraded routes. Identify any boundary where responsibility becomes ambiguous or the same fact is recreated.

### 3. Identify the required capabilities

Use the catalogue below and the operational coverage model. Record the person, service, supplier or automated capability that currently represents each material responsibility.

### 4. Define the two-way exchange

State what Operations needs from the interface and what the interface needs from Operations. Include definitions, evidence quality, timing, confidentiality and acknowledgement.

### 5. Make authority visible

Separate advice, performance, approval, funding, contractual commitment, legal interpretation, risk acceptance and stop authority. A person receiving work does not automatically acquire every form of authority connected to it.

### 6. Test failure and conflict

Ask what happens if the capability is unavailable, late, disagrees, rejects the request, lacks evidence or has a conflicting objective. Define escalation and a proportionate degraded route.

### 7. Observe and improve

Use outcome, delay, rework, failure, dispute, user-experience and feedback evidence to review the interface. Make feedback easy to provide and receive through a suitable form or channel. Automation may support capture, routing, reminders and synthesis where authorised, but the relevant human retains authority over consequential decisions. Improve the combined system rather than optimising one side of the boundary.

## Interface catalogue

### Strategy and organisational direction

- **Why:** connect operational reality and user outcomes to purpose, priorities, policy and the target operating model.
- **When:** strategy changes; priorities conflict; an outcome requires material trade-offs; the operation cannot meet a stated objective; or an opportunity changes the operating model.
- **Inputs from Operations:** demand and outcome evidence, capability and capacity, dependencies, risks, options, assumptions and affected people.
- **Outputs to Operations:** intended outcomes, priority decisions, policy boundaries, investment direction, tolerances and review triggers.
- **Authority:** the authorised strategic owner decides organisational direction; Operations owns accurate operational evidence and delivery within its mandate.
- **Escalate when:** value priorities conflict, objectives are not feasible within known constraints, material consequences sit outside operational authority or nobody owns the end-to-end outcome.

### Finance and financial control

- **Why:** make affordability, resource commitments, financial exposure and realised benefits visible.
- **When:** spend, funding, pricing, revenue, financial reporting, cost transfer, fraud exposure or material benefit assumptions are involved.
- **Inputs from Operations:** volumes, resource use, cost and benefit assumptions, options, uncertainty, failure exposure and implementation timing.
- **Outputs to Operations:** cost model, budget position, financial controls, funding decision, reporting treatment and variance thresholds.
- **Authority:** the relevant budget or financial authority commits funds and approves financial treatment; Operations does not infer spending authority from an operational recommendation.
- **Escalate when:** expenditure is unapproved, assumptions are materially uncertain, benefits are not realised, a variance breaches tolerance or fraud or financial misstatement is suspected.

### People, capability and employment

- **Why:** ensure that roles, capacity, skills, incentives, wellbeing and employment consequences support the intended outcome.
- **When:** work, workload, roles, location, skills, performance expectations, workforce numbers, employee information or human review responsibilities change.
- **Inputs from Operations:** current and residual work, demand, capability gaps, judgement points, workload, safety and wellbeing signals, proposed role change and transition timing.
- **Outputs to Operations:** role and competence requirements, workforce options, learning and support, consultation route, employment controls and approved people decisions.
- **Authority:** authorised leaders and people specialists decide employment and workforce matters; individuals retain rights and routes to question or escalate.
- **Escalate when:** required competence is absent, workload is unsafe, consultation or employment obligations may apply, incentives undermine controls or a proposed automation change hides workforce consequences.

### Legal and contractual interpretation

- **Why:** connect applicable law, rights, duties, liabilities and contracts to operational choices.
- **When:** an obligation is unclear; rights or liability may be affected; a contract is created, changed or disputed; litigation is possible; or specialist legal interpretation is required.
- **Inputs from Operations:** accurate facts, dates, jurisdictions, affected people, records, proposed action, alternatives, urgency and relevant contracts or policies.
- **Outputs to Operations:** legal interpretation or referral, required constraints, decision options, contractual language, evidence needs and deadlines.
- **Authority:** an appropriately authorised legal or contractual decision-maker provides specialist interpretation; Operations Automated does not provide or imply legal approval.
- **Escalate when:** a credible breach, legal deadline, dispute, regulatory exposure, rights impact or conflict between operational preference and legal constraint is identified.

### Risk and compliance

- **Why:** connect operational uncertainty, obligations, controls and decisions to the applicable risk and compliance system.
- **When:** material harm is possible; appetite or tolerance may be exceeded; a control is weak or rejected; an obligation changes; or residual exposure requires acceptance.
- **Inputs from Operations:** affected outcomes and people, scenarios, evidence, likelihood and impact judgement, controls, failures, dependencies, options and uncertainty.
- **Outputs to Operations:** risk record, obligation interpretation, control or treatment requirements, monitoring, acceptance decision, escalation and review trigger.
- **Authority:** risk owners and designated acceptance authorities decide within their mandate; Operations may identify and treat risk but cannot invent acceptance.
- **Escalate when:** potential harm is material, appetite is exceeded, no credible owner exists, evidence is concealed, a required control fails or treatment remains unresolved.

### Security, privacy, identity and access

- **Why:** protect information, people, services and authority from misuse, loss, unauthorised access and unacceptable surveillance.
- **When:** personal or sensitive information is used; systems are connected; access changes; AI or agents receive tools or memory; a threat changes; or a security or privacy incident is suspected.
- **Inputs from Operations:** purpose, data flow, classification, users, roles, systems, permissions, retention, suppliers, threats, recovery need and proposed controls.
- **Outputs to Operations:** security and privacy requirements, access model, assessment, approved controls, monitoring, incident route, retention decision and residual risks.
- **Authority:** authorised security, privacy and information owners approve within their responsibilities; technical ability does not create permission.
- **Escalate when:** unauthorised access or disclosure is suspected, processing lacks a valid basis, the proposed access is excessive, a control cannot be met or incident notification may be required.

### Data, information, records and knowledge

- **Why:** ensure that decisions and work use understandable, reliable, available and appropriately governed information.
- **When:** data is created, combined, reused, reported, migrated or automated; definitions conflict; quality affects a decision; or knowledge becomes a single point of failure.
- **Inputs from Operations:** required decisions and measures, data items, definitions, sources, quality issues, timing, users, retention needs and current workarounds.
- **Outputs to Operations:** agreed definitions, ownership, source and lineage, quality rules, access, retention, knowledge article or procedure, and correction route.
- **Authority:** data, record and knowledge owners decide permitted use and authoritative definitions; users remain responsible for reporting known limitations.
- **Escalate when:** provenance is missing, material definitions conflict, data is insufficient for the consequence, retention or access is unclear or a record cannot be corrected.

### Technology and architecture

- **Why:** provide feasible, supportable, secure and resilient technology that serves the operational outcome.
- **When:** a system, integration, automation, platform, infrastructure component or material technical change is proposed or fails.
- **Inputs from Operations:** user and service needs, work types, volumes, performance, availability, data, controls, exception and recovery requirements, constraints and options.
- **Outputs to Operations:** architecture and implementation options, dependency model, non-functional requirements, technical controls, support model, monitoring, cost and lifecycle implications.
- **Authority:** authorised architecture, engineering and change responsibilities approve technical design and production change; the operational owner retains accountability for the service outcome.
- **Escalate when:** the design creates an unsupported dependency, technical debt prevents a minimum outcome, recovery is unproven, environments diverge materially or the change exceeds technical authority.

### Product, portfolio, programme and change

- **Why:** align competing demand, product or service direction, delivery capacity and cumulative change with operational value.
- **When:** work enters a roadmap or portfolio; several changes compete; a release affects live work; a benefit needs ownership; or cross-team coordination is required.
- **Inputs from Operations:** user need, operational evidence, priority, acceptance criteria, dependencies, readiness, risks, capacity and transition requirements.
- **Outputs to Operations:** portfolio decision, sponsor or product ownership, roadmap, delivery plan, release scope, acceptance route, benefit owner and change communication.
- **Authority:** authorised product, portfolio, programme or change owners decide scope and sequencing within their mandate; operational acceptance and risk authority remain explicit.
- **Escalate when:** competing priorities threaten minimum outcomes, cumulative change exceeds capacity, no sponsor owns the result, acceptance criteria are absent or delivery declares completion without first use.

### Procurement, suppliers and partners

- **Why:** acquire and govern external capability without transferring away accountability for the operational outcome.
- **When:** an external good, service, platform, adviser or subcontractor is selected, changed, renewed, monitored, disputed or exited.
- **Inputs from Operations:** outcome and service requirements, volumes, data and access, controls, performance, resilience, support, location, sustainability and exit needs.
- **Outputs to Operations:** sourcing route, due diligence, contract and service levels, responsibilities, assurance evidence, issue route, continuity and exit plan.
- **Authority:** authorised procurement and contract owners make commercial commitments; operational owners define and monitor the required outcome.
- **Escalate when:** there is concentration or lock-in, a supplier misses a material outcome, rights or data are unclear, assurance is inadequate, subcontracting changes or no viable exit exists.

### Sales, marketing and customer relationships

- **Why:** connect promises, demand, market or user insight and communications to what the operation can deliver.
- **When:** an offer, campaign, commitment, service level, forecast, customer communication or material feedback affects operational work.
- **Inputs from Operations:** capability, capacity, constraints, evidence, service performance, incident status, eligibility, lead time and recovery position.
- **Outputs to Operations:** demand forecast, customer need and feedback, approved promise, communication, segmentation, expectation and relationship decision.
- **Authority:** authorised commercial or relationship owners make external claims and commitments; Operations confirms what can be delivered and identifies risk.
- **Escalate when:** a promise exceeds capability, marketing evidence is unsupported, demand may overwhelm the operation, communications could mislead or customer harm is recurring.

### Audit, quality and independent assurance

- **Why:** provide proportionate confidence that requirements, outcomes and controls are understood and working.
- **When:** consequence warrants independent review; control effectiveness is uncertain; a certification or assurance claim is considered; failures repeat; or an audit is scheduled or triggered.
- **Inputs from Operations:** criteria, process and control design, records, samples, outcomes, exceptions, prior findings, limitations and management assertions.
- **Outputs to Operations:** assurance conclusion, quality result, findings, evidence gaps, corrective actions, owner, due date and follow-up requirement.
- **Authority:** competent assurance or audit responsibilities determine their conclusion independently; management owns treatment and may not edit away an adverse finding.
- **Escalate when:** a systemic or material failure is found, evidence is withheld, independence is compromised, overdue action leaves material exposure or an unsupported assurance claim is made.

### Facilities, workplace, health and safety

- **Why:** provide a safe, accessible and suitable physical environment, equipment and emergency capability.
- **When:** work location, layout, occupancy, equipment, maintenance, physical access, hazard, emergency arrangement or field activity changes.
- **Inputs from Operations:** work patterns, people affected, equipment and space needs, incidents, hazards, accessibility needs, dependencies, capacity and proposed change.
- **Outputs to Operations:** workplace or facilities requirements, risk controls, maintenance, access, emergency plan, competent-person advice and inspection evidence.
- **Authority:** authorised facilities and competent health-and-safety responsibilities decide within their remit; anyone should be able to stop or escalate immediate danger under the applicable arrangements.
- **Escalate when:** immediate danger exists, a statutory report may be required, equipment or premises are unsafe, accessibility prevents use or emergency arrangements cannot sustain minimum outcomes.

### Sustainability and environmental or social responsibility

- **Why:** make environmental, resource and longer-term social effects part of operational value and obligation decisions.
- **When:** energy, materials, waste, travel, sourcing, physical assets, emissions, environmental claims or community effects materially change.
- **Inputs from Operations:** activity and resource data, lifecycle impacts, supplier information, affected communities, options, uncertainty and operational constraints.
- **Outputs to Operations:** materiality decision, requirements, targets, measures, mitigations, supplier conditions, reporting evidence and review triggers.
- **Authority:** authorised sustainability, environmental and organisational leaders set commitments; statutory or specialist decisions remain with the appropriate authority.
- **Escalate when:** a legal threshold may be breached, a public claim lacks evidence, a material impact is hidden or transferred, supplier information is unreliable or the proposed change conflicts with an approved commitment.

## Interface authority rules

- Authority follows the consequence and decision, not a universal title.
- Advice is not approval, and consultation is not transfer of accountability.
- Supplying data does not make the data owner responsible for the operational decision that uses it.
- An operational owner cannot bypass specialist authority merely because a deadline is urgent.
- A specialist function should explain the constraint and available route, not leave the operation with an unexplained rejection.
- Rejection or delay of a treatment does not close the underlying need or risk.
- Automation may route, validate and record an exchange only within explicit permissions and with a visible recovery route.
- Material decisions should record the evidence, authority, reasoning, conditions and review trigger.

## Small organisations and combined roles

In an individual or small organisation, the same person may represent strategy, finance, operations and customer relationships. Use the interface questions as a disciplined change of responsibility rather than manufacturing departments.

Where the person lacks authority, competence or credible independence:

- obtain an external or shared specialist;
- reduce the scope or consequence;
- retain human review and a safe fallback;
- defer the decision while preserving the current risk or need; or
- escalate to the person who can authorise and own the consequence.

Record important conflicts of interest. Self-review may be proportionate for a small reversible change, but it is not independent assurance merely because a checklist was completed.

## Minimum useful output

A proportionate interface review should leave:

- the primary customer, service user or stakeholder journey;
- the end-to-end outcome and accountable owner;
- the material interfaces and the capabilities representing them;
- triggers, inputs, outputs and response expectations;
- decisions and authority boundaries;
- information, control and dependency gaps;
- escalation and recovery routes;
- measures or evidence of interface effectiveness;
- an accessible feedback route with visible ownership and response expectations; and
- one governed next action and review trigger.

Use the [operational coverage and interface map](../templates/operational-coverage-and-interface-map.md) to retain the result.
