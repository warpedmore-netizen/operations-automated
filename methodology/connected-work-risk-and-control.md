# Connected work, risk and control

This proposed module helps an individual, team or organisation connect operational demand, work, causes, uncertainty, decisions and improvement without forcing every setting into the same terminology or governance structure.

It supplements the approved [operational lenses](operational-lenses.md), [readiness path](readiness-path.md) and [OPERATE cycle](operate-overview.md). It remains proposed pending further internal validation and Jamie Peppard's explicit decision.

## Start outside-in

Begin with the outcome, person or organisation affected and the original operational need. Do not begin by assuming that an internal process label explains the whole situation.

Ask proportionately:

- What outcome was sought or placed at risk?
- Who raised, experienced or detected the need?
- Which service, activity or dependency is involved?
- What work types and records already exist?
- Which consequences, obligations and authority boundaries apply?

## Connected work records

| Record | Operational purpose | Typical relationship |
|---|---|---|
| Case | Retain the overall need, interaction, context and history for a person, organisation or defined matter | May link several requests, incidents, decisions and communications |
| Request | Record something being asked for, supplied, changed or explained | May sit within a case or exist as independent operational demand |
| Incident | Record an unplanned interruption, degradation, harmful event or loss of expected service | May affect several cases or services and may contribute to a problem |
| Problem | Investigate an underlying, recurring or systemic condition | May be evidenced by one serious incident, several incidents, failed demand or another signal |
| Risk | Record uncertainty or a condition that could affect intended outcomes | May arise from a problem, dependency, change, third party, decision or external condition |
| Control or change | Prevent, detect, correct, recover from or otherwise treat a condition | Should link to the intended outcome, relevant risk and evidence of effectiveness |
| Decision | Retain a material judgement, authority, rationale, conditions and review trigger | May approve, reject, defer or constrain a priority, treatment, design or delegation |
| Learning | Retain what an outcome, test, failure or review changes | Should alter a decision, control, capability, method or future action where justified |

These records form a network, not a compulsory linear hierarchy. A customer case may create several incidents and requests, but an incident can also be detected by monitoring without a customer case. A risk may exist before its cause is understood. Local terminology may be retained when the intended relationship and evidence remain clear.

## Risk-management connection

When Operations Automated identifies a credible risk, it should:

1. Link to the applicable organisational risk record and owner where one exists.
2. Create a proportionate working record if no credible record exists and the user has authority to do so.
3. Describe the affected outcomes, people, services, dependencies and obligations.
4. Separate evidence, judgement, inference and assumption.
5. Assess likelihood, impact, current controls, uncertainty and urgency using the applicable risk approach.
6. Develop treatment, acceptance, transfer, monitoring and resilience options.
7. Send consequential decisions to the authority appropriate to their effect.
8. Retain the decision, rationale, conditions, residual exposure and review trigger.
9. Test whether the operation can sustain its defined minimum outcomes if the preferred treatment is rejected, delayed or fails.

The methodology does not replace an applicable risk-management, legal, safety, security, continuity or specialist framework. It checks that relevant risk information is connected to the operational decision and does not disappear between records or teams.

## A rejected treatment is not a closed risk

Declining or deferring a control changes the treatment plan; it does not by itself remove the underlying risk.

The risk remains governed until it is treated, transferred, formally accepted or closed under the applicable process. The retained record should show:

- The treatment considered and why it was rejected or deferred
- Who had authority for that decision
- The remaining exposure and people or outcomes affected
- Interim controls, degraded operation or recovery arrangements
- Escalation, monitoring and review triggers

If nobody has credible ownership or authority, record that as an operational-control gap rather than inventing approval.

## Decision evidence

Material decisions that change value priorities, risk exposure, controls, resilience, delegated authority or consequential commitments require a retained record regardless of organisational size.

Proportionality changes the depth and format, not the need for evidence. A small reversible decision may be a dated note in the work record. A high-consequence or cross-organisational decision may require a formal risk record, approval route and assurance pack.

Routine low-consequence operating choices do not each require a separate governance document when the existing case, work log or activity history provides sufficient evidence.

## Accountability and authority

- Anyone may identify a possible problem, risk or critical outcome.
- A person or role should be accountable for ensuring that each material risk is actively managed.
- Identification, assessment, priority, treatment funding and residual-risk acceptance may require different authority.
- Authority follows the consequence of the decision rather than a universal job title.
- A named owner is not an effective control unless they have sufficient information, capability, time and escalation access.

Operations Automated may prepare evidence and recommendations. It does not accept operational risk, commit funding or overrule the authorised user or organisation.

## Use through OPERATE

| Stage | Connected-work application |
|---|---|
| Observe | Connect the original need, cases, demand, incidents, existing risks, controls and real outcomes |
| Prioritise | Compare value, material harm, frequency, systemic effect, effort, reversibility and authority |
| Examine | Investigate problems, dependencies, decision rules, evidence quality and control performance |
| Redesign | Define target work routes, record relationships, ownership, controls, escalation and recovery |
| Automate | Decide which bounded work may be delegated and keep risk, assurance and decision evidence visible |
| Test | Exercise normal, exceptional, harmful and degraded conditions and test control effectiveness |
| Evolve | Retain decisions, unresolved risks, lessons, changed controls and review triggers |

## Minimum useful output

A proportionate application should leave the user with:

- The original need and intended outcome
- Connected work types and system boundaries
- Important incidents, problems, risks and dependencies
- Current controls and evidence of their effectiveness
- Material decisions, owners and authority gaps
- Treatment and resilience options with trade-offs
- A governed next action and review trigger

The output should be understandable without requiring the user to know IT service-management or risk terminology in advance.
