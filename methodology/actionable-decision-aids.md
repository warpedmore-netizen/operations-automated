# Actionable and self-guided decision aids

Operations Automated should help the user progress, not merely describe what they should think about or make them dependent on another conversation.

This proposed module extends the approved [methodology output contract](output-contract.md) and [proportionate delivery modes](proportionate-application-and-delivery-modes.md). It remains proposed pending further internal validation and Jamie Peppard's explicit decision.

## From guidance to usable output

When a repeatable structure can materially help the user reach an answer, a proportionate response should offer or produce an actionable decision aid alongside its analysis.

Possible aids include:

- A short checklist for a bounded question
- A worksheet for collecting and separating material evidence
- A value matrix or category comparison
- A transparent formula or scoring model
- A worked example with an editable blank version
- A decision, risk, control or learning record
- A prioritised action or validation plan

The aid should let the user continue independently. Returning to Operations Automated may add challenge, synthesis or deeper assessment, but should not be required merely because the initial response withheld the means of applying its guidance.

## Select the smallest useful form

Do not turn every question into a spreadsheet or long template. Select the form according to:

- The user's intended outcome and preferred way of working
- Whether evidence must be collected or compared repeatedly
- The number and material difference of the options
- Consequence, uncertainty and reversibility
- Whether a retained decision or approval record is required
- The effort of completing the aid compared with the value it may create

A short answer may be sufficient for a simple, well-supported question. A checklist may be better than a formula. A worksheet becomes useful when the user needs to organise several categories, inputs, trade-offs or controls.

## Categorise before calculating

Where work types differ materially, categorise them before scoring or recommending improvement.

A category should contain work with broadly similar:

- Trigger and intended outcome
- Decision rules and required evidence
- Consequence and reversibility
- Exceptions and escalation route
- Authority and control requirements

If these differ, split the category. A precise calculation against mixed work creates false confidence.

## Transparent calculation

A formula or score is a decision aid, not evidence or approval. It should expose:

- The user's value proposition and weights
- Each input, definition, source and known limitation
- How ratings and calculations combine
- Which assumptions are editable
- Which gates override or constrain the score
- How to interpret the result and what it cannot prove

Avoid unexplained proprietary scores and unnecessary decimal precision. Test sensitivity where a small change in an assumption would reverse the suggested route.

User-defined weights do not remove applicable legal, safety, ethical, human, financial or specialist obligations. Authority, minimum outcomes, recovery and other material controls should remain visible gates rather than disappear inside a weighted average.

## Minimum self-guided package

Where Operations Automated produces a reusable aid, include proportionately:

1. What decision or outcome the aid supports.
2. How to use it in plain language.
3. Definitions and rating guidance.
4. The evidence or inputs required and why they matter.
5. A worked example where it reduces ambiguity.
6. An editable area for the user's own information.
7. Transparent results and their limitations.
8. Missing evidence, failed gates and the next available action.
9. The human control point and retained decision location.
10. A route to export, reuse or continue into deeper assessment without re-entering the work.

## Product behaviour

A future delivery product should be able to generate, retain and export the right aid from the conversation or project context. The user should be able to inspect how it was derived and edit authorised inputs without silently changing methodology rules.

Templates and calculations should be versioned with the methodology that produced them. A later response should be able to analyse the completed aid while distinguishing user evidence, user judgement, AI inference and formula output.

## Validation questions

- Can a person use the aid without facilitation?
- Does it improve the decision or only make the response look more substantial?
- Are the categories, inputs and rating definitions understood consistently?
- Do gates prevent a high score from being mistaken for authority or safety?
- Is the aid proportionate to the decision and user effort?
- Can its output continue into an assessment without rework?
