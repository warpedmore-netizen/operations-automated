# 04 — Improve and implement

## Purpose and intended reader

This chapter helps a reader move from a defined problem or authorised target outcome to a tested, usable and retained improvement. It is for operational owners, problem solvers, change and delivery teams, technology practitioners and people responsible for adoption or assurance.

## Questions this chapter answers

- What should change, and what should remain unchanged?
- How should normal, exceptional, escalation, degraded and recovery routes work?
- What should people, conventional technology, automation, AI or agents handle?
- What must be tested before use and who can accept the result?
- Can the intended user reach, understand and begin using the deliverable?
- What outcome, decision or learning must be retained?

## Topics

- OPERATE: Observe, Prioritise, Examine, Redesign, Automate, Test and Evolve
- Outside-in redesign from the intended journey and outcome
- Minimum useful structure followed by adopt, adapt, test and evolve
- Target operating design and credible alternatives
- Implementation planning, ownership, sequencing and dependencies
- Human roles, capability, controls and authority
- Technology and delegation decisions
- Normal, exceptional, adverse and degraded testing
- Activation, handover and first useful action
- Measures, review triggers and retained learning

## Expected inputs

- Current-state findings and cause hypotheses
- The primary journey, operational injection points and experience evidence
- Prioritised opportunity and authorised target outcome
- Value, minimum outcomes, constraints and affected people
- Readiness gaps and implementation prerequisites
- Options, trade-offs, dependencies and resource constraints
- Risk, control, resilience and recovery requirements
- Named implementation, decision and risk owners

## Outputs

- Target operational design
- A future journey and the minimum operational structure needed to support it
- Implementation and transition route
- Human, automation, AI and agent responsibility model where relevant
- Controls, escalation, degraded operation and recovery design
- Test plan and observed evidence
- Activation and first-use evidence
- Acceptance, rejection or conditional decision
- Outcome measures, retained learning and review trigger
- A receiver-centred feedback loop with ownership, response and improvement routes

## Interfaces and hand-offs

| Interface | What it contributes | What it receives |
|---|---|---|

| Product, engineering and architecture | Design, feasibility, build and lifecycle evidence | Operational requirements, exceptions, human controls and recovery needs |
| People, training and communications | Capability, adoption, consultation and communication | Changed roles, learning needs and first-use route |

| Finance and procurement | Funding, contracting and supplier conditions | Benefits, lifecycle cost, dependency and exit requirements |

## Known gaps

- No detailed implementation-plan or target-operating-model guide exists.
- Organisational change, adoption, communications and training remain underdeveloped.
- Operational acceptance, transition into live work and support hand-off need a defined practice.
- Benefits realisation and post-implementation review lack a complete guide.
- Testing needs examples for physical, human, digital and cross-organisational operations.
- Rollback, degraded operation and supplier failure need deeper patterns.
