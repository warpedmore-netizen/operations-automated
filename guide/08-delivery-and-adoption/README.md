# 08 — Delivery and adoption

## Purpose and intended reader

This chapter helps a reader turn methodology reasoning into an understandable, usable result and support the change into normal work. It is for facilitators, operational owners, product and delivery teams, trainers, support roles and anyone responsible for whether the intended user receives value.

## Questions this chapter answers

- Should the interaction be a quick answer, a self-guided aid or a retained assessment or project?
- What must be returned after each meaningful user contribution?
- Which format is the smallest one that improves understanding or action?
- Can the intended user reach, understand and complete the first useful action?
- What training, communication, support and role transition are needed?
- How will confusion, rejection, abandonment or failed activation be observed and corrected?

## Topics

- Ask and assessment/project delivery modes
- Minimum response and evidence contract
- Progressive outputs rather than question-only intake
- Smallest useful decision aid and optional deeper structure
- Human-AI collaboration and representation choice
- Activation, first use and visible progress
- Adoption, training, communications and support
- Receiver-centred feedback in the form and channel that work for the person
- Automated feedback capture, routing and synthesis within human-controlled boundaries
- Outcome observation and continued improvement

## Expected inputs

- Reader route, intended user and desired outcome
- Current evidence, analysis and recommendation
- User's environment, capability, accessibility and time constraints
- Required artefact, decision or operational action
- Delivery channel, permissions and dependencies
- The user's preferred or accessible form, channel and feedback effort
- Consequence, reversibility and human control point
- Adoption, support and recovery needs

## Outputs

- Proportionate answer, assessment or implementation artefact
- Plain user guide and explanation of limitations
- Visible facts, judgement, inference, assumptions and recommendation
- Activation and first-use route
- Training, communication or support actions where required
- Progress, failure and recovery signals
- Retained result and continuation route
- Accessible feedback route, acknowledgement and response expectation
- Feedback ownership, governed improvement route and outcome-review trigger

## Interfaces and hand-offs

| Interface | What it contributes | What it receives |
|---|---|---|
| Intended users and affected people | Context, accessibility, comprehension and outcome evidence | Usable output, limitations, control point and feedback route |
| Operational owner | Purpose, authority and acceptance criteria | Delivery evidence, adoption position and unresolved risk |
| Product and experience design | Interaction, navigation and accessibility | Method rules, output contract and user evidence |
| Training, people and communications | Capability, role transition and communication channels | Changed-work requirements and learning outcomes |
| Support and service teams | Environment, incident, recovery and recurring-friction evidence | First-use guide, escalation and known limitations |
| Technology, data and security | Delivery capability, access and information controls | Purpose, permitted information flow and recovery requirements |

## Known gaps

- No complete facilitation guide exists for quick questions or full assessments.
- Accessibility, language level, different learning preferences and assistive technology need explicit guidance.
- Adoption, consultation, training, communications and support practices are not developed in depth.
- Independent-user evidence for decision aids and first use remains limited.
- Product journeys have not yet reproduced genuine AI-assisted analysis reliably.
- Time to first value, abandonment, comprehension and support burden need defined measures.
- Feedback mechanisms need testing across different users, channels, accessibility needs and consequences.
