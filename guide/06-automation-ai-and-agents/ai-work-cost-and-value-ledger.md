# AI work, cost and value ledger

> **Use this guide when** a material AI-enabled workflow, AI tool or internally created AI application needs a defensible scale, change, replace or stop decision.

This proposed ledger joins operational purpose, ownership, technology, lifecycle, full cost and observed outcomes. It is not an adoption register, surveillance tool, provider dashboard or financial approval.

## 1. Status and applicability

Use the ledger for material work, not every experiment. It is C2 until one non-confidential case shows that an owner can maintain it and make a decision that adoption or token totals alone would miss.

## 2. Purpose and intended outcomes

The ledger should make one AI-enabled operation understandable enough to answer:

- what outcome and person justify the use;
- who owns the operation and resulting decision;
- which provider, model, tool, data and internal software are involved;
- what the full comparison cost is;
- what benefit, harm and transferred work are observed; and
- whether to scale, change, replace, retain experimentally or stop.

## 3. Intended reader, participants and roles

The operational owner defines the outcome and owns the service decision. Finance challenges cost and attribution. Technology, data, security, privacy and procurement provide evidence within their authority. Users and affected people supply experience and unintended-effect evidence. AI may analyse but cannot validate its own value or approve continuation.

## 4. When to use it

Use before a material pilot, at the pilot review, after a provider/model or architecture change, when cost or volume changes materially, after an incident, and at retirement. Use a 30- to 60-day observation period only where that duration is proportionate to the work and decision.

## 5. Scope, boundaries and non-goals

Include approved, experimental, embedded and discovered shadow use without implying approval. Exclude personal monitoring, confidential source material and exhaustive low-value inventory. Do not claim precise attribution where evidence cannot support it.

## 6. Concepts and terminology

- **Outcome unit:** the smallest meaningful completed outcome, such as a resolved eligible case, accepted analysis or successfully supported decision.
- **Baseline:** the credible comparison before the AI-enabled route, including quality, effort, delay, failure and cost.
- **Full cost:** provider and infrastructure cost plus material human, engineering, assurance, support, rework, failure and exit effort.
- **Transferred work:** effort removed from one role but created elsewhere.
- **AI-built internal application:** software materially created or changed with AI assistance that still needs an owner, acceptance, support, recovery and retirement route.

## 8. Inputs, evidence and entry criteria

Use authorised, proportionate sources. Where relevant reconcile at least four source types: procurement or expense, identity/access, technology/network/API evidence and practitioner declaration. A source may reveal use without proving legitimacy, value or risk acceptance.

## 9. Demand, work types and variation

Separate materially different work before calculating averages: routine and exceptional work, high- and low-consequence work, assisted and unassisted work, accepted and rejected output, new and reworked work. One aggregate can hide poor outcomes or cost transfer.

## 10. Activities and workflow

1. Start from the primary journey and define the outcome unit.
2. Establish the baseline and credible non-AI alternative.
3. Set materiality and inclusion rules.
4. Identify the workflow, owner, users, provider/model/tool, data/access and internal software lifecycle.
5. Reconcile available discovery sources and label unknown or unapproved use.
6. Measure full cost and outcomes over a proportionate period.
7. Compare quality, risk, latency, portability, support and cost.
8. Surface benefit, harm, transferred effort and uncertainty.
9. Make a scale/change/replace/experimental/stop recommendation.
10. Record the authorised decision, conditions and review trigger.

## 11. Decision rights, rules and escalation

Discovery creates a review item, not approval or disciplinary evidence. The operational owner recommends the service decision; Finance, Technology, Security, Data, Procurement, Risk or specialist roles decide within their authority. Spending, connection, access, risk acceptance and external communication remain explicit human decisions.

## 13. Interfaces and dependencies

Material interfaces normally include the operational owner, Finance, Technology/Engineering, Data/Knowledge, Security/Privacy, Procurement/Supplier, Product or service lifecycle, and affected users. Record the information supplied, decision or evidence returned, owner, failure signal and recovery route for each used interface.

## 14. People, capability and accessibility

Record whose effort is reduced, increased or made more difficult. Include review competence, support burden, capability transfer, accessibility and degraded-operation training. Do not count an avoided task as a benefit if the same work reappears as correction or support.

## 15. Information, data and knowledge

Record source, permission, quality, ownership, retention and limitations. Provider billing is cost evidence, not outcome evidence. Identity or network records may show use but not purpose. Practitioner declarations require a safe, non-punitive route if the objective is accurate discovery.

## 16. Risks, controls and obligations

Consider shadow use, sensitive data, shared credentials, unsupported internal software, supplier concentration, model change, false attribution, cost escalation, lock-in, poor-quality scaling, user harm and surveillance. Connect applicable specialist obligations without claiming compliance.

## 18. Measures, performance, feedback and learning

Prefer outcome, quality, effort, risk, experience and cost-per-accepted-outcome measures. Adoption, generated content, model calls and token reduction are supporting activity measures only. Record when evidence expires and who receives and responds to feedback.

## 19. Failure, resilience and recovery

Test provider or model unavailability, unexpected cost growth, quality deterioration, missing data, revoked access, internal-application failure and exit. Record the minimum service, fallback, owner, recovery evidence and effect on the primary journey.

## 20. Tailoring, implementation, example and known gaps

Use a single-page ledger for one low-volume workflow; add detailed finance, supplier or lifecycle evidence only where consequence warrants it. The first case should use real but non-confidential figures or defensible ranges. Known gaps include shared-cost allocation, long-term outcome attribution, provider comparability and standard AI unit economics.
