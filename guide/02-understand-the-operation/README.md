# 02 — Understand the operation

## Purpose and intended reader

This chapter helps a reader understand how work really happens as a connected system before selecting an improvement or technology. It is for problem solvers, operational owners, practitioners and affected specialists investigating an activity, process, service, function or operating model.

## Questions this chapter answers

- What outcome is sought and what demand enters the operation?
- Who performs, receives, depends on or is affected by the work?
- How do work, information and decisions flow in reality?
- Which cases, requests, incidents, problems, risks, controls and decisions are connected?
- Where are queues, failure demand, workarounds, dependencies and single points of failure?
- What evidence is reliable and what remains inference or assumption?

## Topics

- System purpose, boundary and operating context
- Outside-in customer, service user or stakeholder journeys
- The movement from the journey into the operational value system
- Operational injection points that support each journey step
- Products, services and processes as operational contexts rather than assumed starting points
- Required outcomes, minimum outcomes and MoSCoW scope where useful
- Nine connected operational lenses
- People, experience and human judgement
- Demand, work types, variation and exceptions
- Flow, hand-offs, queues, capacity and dependencies
- Ownership, decisions and knowledge
- Cases, requests, incidents, problems, risks, controls, changes and learning
- Information, data, technology, performance and current evidence

## Expected inputs

- The foundation outputs: purpose, value, scope, affected people and authority
- The primary person's trigger, journey, expected experience and intended outcome
- Examples of normal, exceptional, failed and recovered work
- Available demand, volume, quality, timeliness and outcome evidence
- Existing processes, work records, policies, controls and measures
- Practitioner and user judgement, identified as judgement
- Upstream, downstream, supplier and system dependencies
- Known uncertainty, contradictions and missing evidence

## Outputs

- A connected current-state operational picture
- An outside-in journey showing visible experience and supporting operational injection points
- An end-to-end operational value-system map beginning with the first operational event
- Required outcomes, exclusions and minimum-outcome gates
- Demand and work-type profile
- Flow and dependency map
- Ownership, decision and knowledge view
- Connected work-record model
- Risk, control and resilience observations
- Information and evidence gaps
- A concise statement of what the current evidence supports

## Interfaces and hand-offs

| Interface | Information received | Information provided |
|---|---|---|
| Customers, sales, product and service teams | Demand, promises, experience and outcome expectations | Demand patterns, failure points and operational feasibility |
| People, workforce and training | Capacity, skill, role and work-as-done evidence | Capability gaps, workload and changed-role implications |
| Technology, data and security | Systems, data definitions, constraints and incidents | Dependencies, information requirements and failure effects |
| Suppliers and procurement | Service commitments, dependencies and contract boundaries | Observed performance, operational risk and exit needs |
| Risk, compliance and audit | Obligations, risks, controls and findings | Connected operational evidence and control-performance questions |
| Finance and planning | Cost, resource and forecast evidence | Demand, capacity, bottleneck and investment context |

## Known gaps

- No detailed discovery or observation guide exists for work-as-done.
- Demand analysis, capacity, queue, hand-off and flow methods remain high level.
- The journey-to-operation sequence and use of MoSCoW or alternative prioritisation methods require case testing.
- The proposed interface template has not yet been tested with an independent user or materially different cases.
- Detailed journey-mapping guidance and varied customer, physical-operation and supplier-network examples remain missing.
- The connected-work model identifies record relationships but does not yet provide complete practice lifecycles.
- Evidence quality and confidence need more practical rating guidance.
