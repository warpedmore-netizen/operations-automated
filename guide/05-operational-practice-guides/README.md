# 05 — Operational practice guides

## Purpose and intended reader

This chapter is the proposed catalogue for detailed operational practice guidance. It is for readers who need to go beyond the nine lenses and apply Operations Automated to a specific discipline, work type or cross-functional relationship.

The catalogue makes missing depth visible. Listing a practice does not claim that the practice is complete, mandatory or owned only by an “operations” team. Practices are connected capabilities that should be selected and tailored according to the outcome, evidence, consequence and environment.

## Questions this chapter answers

- Which operational practice or combination of practices is material to this situation?
- What guidance already exists and how complete is it?
- Which non-operational functions provide essential inputs, authority or capability?
- What outputs, records and interfaces should a mature practice provide?
- Where must an existing specialist framework or obligation take precedence?
- What should be developed next from case evidence?

“Outline only” means the operational lenses mention the topic but no complete practice guide exists. “Partial working guidance” means at least one approved internal module provides useful content but does not yet meet the proposed authoring standard.

## Expected inputs

- The operational question, scope and selected reader route
- Material operational lenses and current-state findings
- Relevant work types, variation, risk and dependencies
- Existing organisational practices and terminology
- Applicable legal, safety, regulatory, security or professional requirements
- Known cross-functional owners and decision rights
- Case evidence showing where generic guidance is insufficient

## Outputs

- Selected practice families and rationale
- A practice coverage and completeness view
- Required roles, inputs, activities, outputs and interfaces
- Identified specialist obligations and boundaries
- Known guidance gaps and the evidence needed to close them
- A proportionate implementation or improvement route

## Interfaces and hand-offs

Every detailed practice guide should define an interface contract:

| Interface field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Provider or partner | The individual, team, function, supplier or system involved |
| What the practice receives | Information, demand, authority, capability, funding or service |
| What the practice provides | Outcome, decision, record, control, service or evidence |
| Owner and authority | Who owns the hand-off and who can decide when it fails |
| Timing or trigger | Entry, completion, escalation and review conditions |
| Failure and recovery | How a failed hand-off is detected, contained and recovered |

Common interfaces include leadership, customers, product, sales, finance, people or HR, legal, compliance, risk, audit, security, data, technology, architecture, projects, change, communications, facilities, procurement and third parties.

## Known gaps

- None of the listed practice families has a complete, independently validated Operations Automated guide.
- Roles, measures, workflows, interface contracts and worked examples are generally missing.
- Outside-in journey mapping, operational injection points and receiver-centred feedback need detailed practice guidance and case evidence.
- Physical operations, non-service settings and smaller individual workflows are underrepresented.
- The boundary between generic guidance and specialist legal, safety, security or professional practice needs examples.
- A prioritised development sequence should follow varied use cases rather than attempting to write every guide at once.
