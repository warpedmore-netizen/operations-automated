# 09 — Use cases

## Purpose and intended reader

This chapter is the approved internal-validation catalogue of materially different scenarios used to test, explain and deepen Operations Automated. It is for methodology users, facilitators, authors, reviewers and product teams who need evidence that the method transfers beyond one service-management or automation example.

The scenarios are non-confidential prompts, not claims of completed validation. A developed case should identify its evidence boundary, separate hypothetical assumptions from observations and retain what the case changes in the methodology.

## Questions this chapter answers

- Does the methodology work for individuals, teams, organisations and networks?
- Does it cover physical, digital, service and non-service operations?
- Can it handle both high-volume routine work and low-volume high-consequence work?
- Does it connect operations with customers, people, finance, suppliers, audit, risk and technology?
- Can it recommend simplification or human work rather than defaulting to automation?
- Which practice guide or methodology gap does each case test?

### Scenario catalogue

|---:|---|---|---|

The catalogue should expand when a new scenario tests a genuinely different boundary, stakeholder, consequence, work type or delivery form. Rephrasing the same automation question is not sufficient variation.

## Expected inputs

- A hypothetical or authorised non-confidential scenario
- Intended outcome, beneficiary and value conflict
- Scale, work type, volume, variation and consequence
- Affected people and cross-functional interfaces
- Available evidence and clearly labelled assumptions
- Relevant authority, obligations and recovery needs
- The methodology proposition or gap being tested

## Outputs

- A case brief and selected reader route
- Proportionate methodology output or artefact
- Evidence, assumptions, uncertainty and decision boundary
- Material practice and interface coverage
- Transfer, reverse, stakeholder, failure or authority challenge
- Retained lesson, no-change reasoning or proposed methodology gap
- Traceability to a feedback record where change may be justified

## Interfaces and hand-offs

| Interface | Role in a use case |
|---|---|
| Scenario owner or practitioner | Confirms real-work plausibility and operational judgement |
| Affected user or stakeholder | Tests value, harm, comprehension and practicality |
| Relevant specialist | Challenges legal, safety, financial, security or professional assumptions |
| Methodology reviewer | Identifies coverage, contradiction, duplication and missing depth |
| Product or delivery reviewer | Tests whether the output is usable and can be activated |
| Authorised human | Decides whether evidence justifies a method change or operational action |

## Known gaps

- Most scenarios are not yet developed or validated.
- Independent practitioners and affected users have not tested the catalogue.
- Physical, safety-critical, public-service, creative and network-of-organisations settings need additional evidence.
- The case format, evidence threshold and comparable outcome measures are not yet standardised.
- Receiver-centred, multi-channel feedback has not yet been tested through a complete improvement loop.
- No case has yet demonstrated the complete loop from use through approved change and observed post-release outcome.
