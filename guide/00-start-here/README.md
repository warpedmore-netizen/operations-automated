# 00 — Start here

## Purpose and intended reader

Use this chapter to enter Operations Automated without first learning the complete methodology. It is for an individual, team or organisation with a question, an operation to assess, an improvement to implement or a decision to govern.

Choose the shortest route that can create a responsible, useful outcome. A quick question should not become an oppressive assessment, while a consequential decision should not be reduced to a convenient checklist.

## Questions this chapter answers

- What is Operations Automated and what is OPERATE's place within it?
- Who is the primary customer, service user, stakeholder or beneficiary, and what journey or outcome matters to them?
- Once that journey is understood, what operational value system must deliver and sustain the outcome?
- Can the current question be answered proportionately from the available information?
- Should the reader use a quick question, full assessment, implementation or governance route?
- What useful output should appear before more information is requested?
- When must the work deepen, stop or return to an earlier point?

## Topics

| Route | Use when | Immediate behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Quick question | A reader needs a bounded explanation, decision structure or next action | Apply the relevant method behind the response and return a useful provisional answer or smallest aid |
| Full assessment | The operation, evidence, dependencies or consequence require a connected view | Define value and boundaries, assess material lenses and form a retained recommendation |
| Implementation | A target outcome or change is ready to be designed, delivered or tested | Use OPERATE proportionately and keep readiness, controls, recovery and authority visible |
| Governance and review | Evidence, risk, approval, release or methodology meaning requires a decision | Retain the trigger, alternatives, authority, decision, conditions and review route |

## Expected inputs

- The question, problem, opportunity or change in ordinary language
- The primary person's trigger, interaction, journey or expected outcome, where known
- The intended outcome and people affected, where known
- The current scope and urgency
- Available evidence and known uncertainty
- The decision or action required now
- Known authority, obligations or information boundaries

The absence of complete information is not a reason to return nothing. State what can already be understood and ask only for information that could materially change the conclusion.

## Outputs

- The selected route and why it is proportionate
- A plain-English current understanding
- The outside-in starting journey and material operational injection points, where relevant
- The first operational event, required outcomes and end-to-end operational value system, where a fuller understanding is needed
- Immediate useful guidance or a partial assessment
- Important evidence, inference, assumptions and gaps
- The next action and human control point
- A clear route to deepen the work without re-entering existing context

## Interfaces and hand-offs

| Interface | What may be needed from it | What Operations Automated provides | Hand-off trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operational owner or individual | Purpose, real-work context and authority | Current understanding, options and next action | A decision or operational commitment is required |
| Affected people or customers | Needs, experience and possible harm | Visible impact and opportunity to challenge assumptions | Their experience could materially change the design |
| Specialist function | Legal, safety, security, financial or professional judgement | Operational context, evidence boundary and precise question | General guidance cannot responsibly determine the issue |
| Delivery or change team | Feasibility, capacity and implementation conditions | Authorised outcome, dependencies, controls and measures | The work moves from assessment into implementation |

## Known gaps

- No concise route-specific worked examples are yet approved.
- Escalation from a quick question into a retained assessment needs repeated case evidence.
- Accessibility and different levels of methodology literacy need deliberate testing.
