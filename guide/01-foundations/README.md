# 01 — Foundations

## Purpose and intended reader

This chapter establishes why Operations Automated exists and the principles that constrain how it is applied. It is for every reader, particularly people setting scope, priorities, authority or the intended value of operational work.

## Questions this chapter answers

- What problem is Operations Automated intended to solve?
- Who may use it and at what scale?
- What does “better” mean in this context and who benefits?
- Whose journey should provide the outside-in starting point?
- Which obligations, minimum outcomes and authority boundaries constrain the work?
- What must remain human-controlled?
- How should failure and novice or pressured use be treated?
- How much structure is useful now, and how should it evolve?

## Topics

- Purpose, intended users and methodology boundaries
- Outside-in design from the primary customer, service user or stakeholder journey
- User-defined value and conflicting outcomes
- Minimum useful structure, adopted and adapted rather than followed as doctrine
- Human-led automation, AI and agents
- Learning through bounded failure
- Receiver-centred feedback and continuous improvement
- TIGIPI as a human-factors heuristic
- Evidence, judgement, accountability and authority
- Proportionate application from an individual activity to an operating system

## Expected inputs

- The operation, activity, service, system or decision in scope
- The primary customer, service user, stakeholder or beneficiary and their journey
- Desired outcome and beneficiaries
- Forms and priorities of value
- Minimum acceptable outcomes and constraints
- People affected, including those who may be disadvantaged
- Decision, risk and implementation authority
- Applicable legal, safety, ethical, human and organisational obligations

## Outputs

- A clear purpose and system boundary
- An outside-in starting journey and its intended experience
- A proportionate value matrix
- Identified beneficiaries and affected people
- Minimum outcomes and non-negotiable constraints
- Visible human authority and accountability
- A reasoned statement of which principles materially shape the work
- The minimum initial structure and feedback loop needed to learn and improve

## Interfaces and hand-offs

| Interface | What it contributes | What this chapter provides |
|---|---|---|
| Leadership and strategy | Direction, priorities and risk appetite | Operational purpose, trade-offs and authority needs |
| Customers, users and communities | Desired outcomes, experience and potential harm | Explicit beneficiary and minimum-outcome view |
| People or HR functions | Roles, capability, consultation, accessibility and wellbeing | Human work and capability implications |
| Legal, compliance, safety and ethics | Binding duties and specialist interpretation | Scope, evidence and operational decision context |
| Finance and investment | Affordability and investment authority | Defined forms of value rather than assumed cost reduction |

- No approved glossary explains core terms consistently for a new reader.
- Practical examples of competing forms of value remain limited.
- Tailoring guidance for individual, team, service and organisation scales needs examples.
- Stakeholder participation, accessibility and consultation methods need deeper guidance.
- The relationship with strategy, portfolio management, finance and organisational design is outlined but not yet developed.
