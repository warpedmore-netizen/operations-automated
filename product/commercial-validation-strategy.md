# Commercial validation strategy

## Commercial objective

Develop scalable revenue from Operations Automated without turning the methodology into an automation sales funnel, overstating evidence or building a costly product before users demonstrate willingness to pay.

The commercial model should support two connected products:

1. **Knowledge product:** guides, examples, templates and maintained methodology content.
2. **Applied product:** an AI-assisted workspace that applies the methodology to a real operation and retains findings, decisions, risks and improvement.

These should form one value ladder rather than two unrelated offers. The guide teaches and proves the method; the applied product reduces the effort required to use it repeatedly.

## Two delivery experiences

The same commercial product may expose two connected experiences:

1. **Ask:** a user asks a question and receives an immediate, proportionate Operations Automated answer with evidence, assumptions, limitations and a next action.
2. **Assessment or project:** the user requests consultancy-style depth, retained evidence, connected operational outputs and an implementation route.

An ask-mode answer should be able to become the first record in a paid assessment or retained workspace. This creates a natural value transition without making the quick answer deliberately incomplete or requiring the user to repeat their context.

The commercial distinction is depth, continuity and retained application—not access to different methodology principles.

## Current market signal

The signal is credible but commercial fit is not yet validated.

- UK government research published in 2026 found that around one in six surveyed businesses used at least one AI technology. Lack of an identified need and limited skills were common barriers; most adopters reported productivity improvement but most had not experienced a revenue change. This supports a need for use-case, readiness and value discipline rather than generic encouragement to adopt AI. [UK AI Adoption Research](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-adoption-research/ai-adoption-research)
- UK government research into advanced-technology adoption found that risk profile, clarity of use case, affordability and regulation strongly influence adoption decisions. These are directly addressed by Operations Automated. [Barriers and Enablers to Advanced Technology Adoption](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/barriers-and-enablers-to-advanced-technology-adoption-for-uk-businesses)
- McKinsey's 2025 global survey associated workflow redesign with the strongest effect on reported EBIT impact from generative AI, while more than 80% of respondents reported no tangible enterprise-level EBIT effect. This supports starting with operating design and measurable outcomes rather than installing AI first. [The State of AI](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai-how-organizations-are-rewiring-to-capture-value)
- Substantial adaptable guidance is already available freely, including the [NIST AI RMF Playbook](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/playbook/). A paid static framework must therefore add applied reasoning, accessibility, connected operational scope, examples, updates or delivery capability.
- Existing providers charge for process documentation, workflow platforms and maintained practice libraries. Scribe offers free and paid process-documentation tiers, while PeopleCert packages books, practice guides, templates, case studies and learning into membership. This shows established paid categories but does not prove demand for Operations Automated. [Scribe pricing](https://scribe.com/pricing), [PeopleCert membership](https://www.peoplecert.org/Membership)

## Proposed positioning

### Short promise

**Improve the operation. Prove the readiness. Automate only what earns it.**

### Candidate unique selling proposition

Operations Automated helps a non-technical problem solver turn a real operational problem into an evidence-based improvement and AI-readiness route, while keeping value, risk, authority and accountability with people.

Its intended differentiation is the combination of:

- Whole-operation improvement rather than isolated process documentation
- One route from operational basics through automation, AI and bounded agents
- Useful analysis and governed next actions rather than a score or checklist
- Connected cases, incidents, requests, problems, risks, controls and decisions
- AI-led execution with explicit human control over meaning and consequence
- Proportionate use across individuals, teams and organisations
- A living methodology that improves through traceable feedback without self-authorising

This is a positioning hypothesis. It becomes a defensible USP only when target users recognise the problem, prefer the promise to alternatives and pay for the result.

## Recommended first market

Begin with a narrow beachhead:

> Operations managers, team leaders, process owners and independent practitioners in small and medium-sized service or back-office operations who are under pressure to improve or adopt AI but do not have a mature transformation function.

This group is close enough to the problem to recognise it, broad enough to supply cases and more accessible than an enterprise procurement route. Independent practitioners may also become users, referrers or licensed delivery partners later.

Do not market to “every individual and every business” initially. The methodology may be widely applicable, but an early sales message needs one recognisable buyer, problem and outcome.

## Product and revenue ladder

| Offer | User outcome | Revenue role | Initial price hypothesis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open diagnostic and selected guidance | Understand the current operational problem and receive one useful next action | Trust, audience and qualified demand | Free |
| Proportionate ask experience | Receive a methodology-based answer and understand whether deeper work is justified | Product discovery, trust and potential conversion | Free allowance or low-cost test after validation |
| Focused guide | Apply one job-to-be-done, such as identifying AI-ready work or connecting cases to risk | Low-support first purchase | Test £39–£79 one-off |
| Practitioner toolkit | Use the method with templates, worked examples, facilitation prompts and updates | Higher-value knowledge product | Test £149–£299 one-off or annual edition |
| Founding-user facilitated assessment | Receive an applied assessment and help validate the method | Evidence and case development; not passive | Test £500–£1,500 per bounded case |
| Individual applied workspace | Receive recurring AI-assisted analysis and retain operational decisions | Recurring self-service revenue | Test £20–£40 per month after validation |
| Team workspace or licence | Share evidence, roles, approvals, risks, reporting and methodology updates | Higher recurring value | Test £150–£500 per month or annual equivalent |
| Business licence and enablement | Govern wider adoption, controlled integrations and internal capability | Later enterprise revenue | Scope and price after evidence |

These are price-test bands, not approved prices. VAT, payment fees, AI usage, support time, security, hosting and refund obligations must be modelled before sale.

## One-off or subscription

A static guide is best sold once. A subscription is justified only if it delivers continuing value such as:

- Maintained methodology releases and new specialist modules
- New worked cases and comparative examples
- Reusable AI-assisted assessments and retained workspaces
- Team collaboration, decisions, risks and reporting
- Regular practitioner challenges or learning sessions
- Controlled updates when technology, standards or accepted practice change

Charging repeatedly for an unchanged document would weaken trust. The application is the stronger recurring-revenue opportunity, but only after the facilitated method demonstrates repeated value.

## Acquisition route

Subject to separate approval for external publication and connections:

1. Publish useful Operations Automated explanations and anonymised hypothetical cases through the existing LinkedIn presence.
2. Lead each piece to one free diagnostic rather than directly to a large methodology sale.
3. Invite diagnostic users to a focused paid guide or founding-user assessment.
4. Retain consented, non-confidential evidence about outcomes, confusion, repeated use and willingness to pay.
5. Use repeated jobs and outputs to specify the applied workspace.

Suggested content themes are:

- Why automating a broken operation does not create value
- How to decide what AI should and should not handle
- How cases, incidents, requests, problems and risks connect
- How to remove ceremonial approval without removing accountability
- What minimum service must survive when automation fails

No publication, contact, mailing-list connection, payment service or sales activity is authorised by this proposal.

## Ninety-day validation sequence

### Phase 1: complete and package the evidence

- Complete the seven-response founder pilot and v0.5 assurance decision.
- Run two different facilitated, non-confidential operational cases.
- Select the one problem for which the method gives the clearest valuable output.

### Phase 2: test the promise before building

- Show the candidate promise and sample output to 10 relevant practitioners.
- Record the words they use for the problem, current alternatives and buying authority.
- Offer a clearly bounded founding-user assessment or guide pre-order.
- Treat payment or a specific purchasing commitment as stronger evidence than positive feedback.

### Phase 3: sell the smallest complete outcome

- Produce one focused guide and toolkit from validated cases.
- Measure completion, time to useful decision, practical outcome, support burden and refund requests.
- Offer the facilitated service only where it continues to generate method and product evidence.

### Phase 4: decide whether recurring software is justified

Build the applied workspace only if users repeatedly need the method, retained records or collaboration and are willing to pay enough to cover AI, hosting, security, support and continued methodology development.

## Validation measures

- Percentage of target users who recognise the problem without lengthy explanation
- Diagnostic completion and next-action usefulness
- Guide or assessment conversion from a specific audience
- Willingness to pay, not only stated interest
- Time to first useful operational decision
- Evidence of an improved outcome within an agreed review period
- Repeat use of the method on another work item
- Support minutes and AI cost per paying user
- Retention and cancellation reasons for any subscription
- Permissioned case evidence and referrals

## Main commercial risks

- The scope sounds broad and generic before a narrow use case is demonstrated.
- Free frameworks and general-purpose AI can reproduce terminology without reproducing reliable applied judgement.
- A downloadable guide may be easy to copy and difficult to update.
- A subscription may create continuous content and support obligations rather than passive income.
- Enterprise customers may require security, privacy, procurement, legal and integration work that changes the economics.
- Founder credibility and audience may become a single point of failure unless evidence, examples and delivery capability become transferable.

## Recommendation

Do not choose between “the document” and “the application” yet. Validate them as a ladder:

1. Free diagnostic to create trust and reveal demand.
2. Paid focused guide and toolkit to test the promise and willingness to pay.
3. Facilitated assessments to create evidence and improve the method.
4. Recurring applied workspace only when repeated use and economics justify it.

The next commercial decision should be the first narrow buyer and job-to-be-done, not the final application feature list.
