# Incident Management Simulation Game

## Original idea or problem

A multiplayer, AI-assisted incident simulation platform where teams practise realistic operational incidents through roles such as incident commander, responder, Risk, Compliance, customer service, communications, observer and evaluator.

The system would:

- adapt scenarios based on participant decisions;
- allow facilitator interventions and new injects;
- capture decisions, delays and escalation points;
- identify gaps in ownership, communication and runbooks;
- generate a debrief with lessons and actions; and
- support the cycle of **Practise → Observe → Debrief → Improve → Re-test**.

Externally, it would be positioned as an incident simulation, operational resilience exercise or team readiness platform rather than solely as a game.

## Why it may be valuable

The idea may turn operational readiness from passive documentation into observable practice. It could reveal decision, ownership, communication, escalation and runbook weaknesses in a bounded setting; retain learning across repeated exercises; and create a differentiated team-level commercial proposition.

These are hypotheses, not validated outcomes. The same learning might initially be achieved faster through a facilitated exercise and lightweight records.

### AI inference

- The strongest early test may be a facilitated, low-technology simulation because it could test the customer problem, roles, exercise loop and value of the debrief before committing to multiplayer platform infrastructure.
- Adaptive AI could create value, but it also introduces scenario-control, evaluation-consistency, security, data and facilitation questions that require evidence.

### Assumptions and limitations

- Teams value repeated incident practice enough to allocate time or budget.
- Adaptive scenarios improve learning rather than create noise or unfair evaluation.
- Useful readiness evidence can be retained without encouraging surveillance or false assurance.
- The concept can be differentiated from existing exercise and simulation offerings.
